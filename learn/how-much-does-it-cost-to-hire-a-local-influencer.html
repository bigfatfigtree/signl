How much does it cost to hire a local influencer?

Most owners ask for a single price and get a confusing range. The honest answer is a total budget view: what you pay in product, what you pay in cash when you choose a paid post, and what a plan costs if you want visits every month. This page adds all three up, with no hidden layer.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 15, 2026 · Magpie Learn hub

The Short Answer

Hiring a local influencer costs $0 in cash for a comped visit, where you pay in your product at your cost, or a paid rate the creator sets starting at $50. On a founding plan, expect $199, $349 or $699 a month for five, ten or twenty visits.

Key Takeaways
  • A comped visit costs $0 in cash, and you pay only your product cost, not the retail price.
  • A paid post is booked at the creator own listed rate, starting at $50, and the creator keeps 97 percent.
  • Founding plans are $199, $349 or $699 a month for 5, 10 or 20 visits, with founding pricing published on the site.
The total view

What are the three ways to pay a local influencer?

Owners usually hear one number at a time, which makes comparison impossible. A creator quotes a rate, an agency quotes a retainer, and a platform quotes a subscription, and none of them include the product you hand over at the door. Start instead with the total: product cost plus cash plus plan, for the number of visits you actually want in a month. That total is the only fair way to compare a comp, a paid post and a plan side by side.

On Magpie there are exactly three ways to pay, and you never mix two of them on the same visit. The first is a comped visit, paid in the meal, service or experience itself. The second is a paid booking at the creator own listed rate. The third is a founding business plan that organizes visits month after month. If you are opening in Nashville or any other market, the math works the same way, because the cost follows your product and your choices, not a national rate card.

Lane one · Comps

What does a comped visit include, and what does it really cost?

A comped visit is the standard way in for most local businesses. You provide the meal, service or experience, the creator visits like a real customer, and the creator posts honestly about what they found. The standard deliverable is 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, posted with the goal of going live within 48 hours of the visit, kept live for 30 or more days, with clear FTC disclosure such as #gifted or a Paid Partnership label. That deliverable is written down before the visit, so both sides know what a finished visit means.

Your cost is your product cost, not the menu or menu board price. A dinner that sells for a higher retail price costs you the ingredients and the time to make it. A service appointment costs you the product used and an hour that might otherwise sit open. You set the comp and its ceiling on every offer, so the cost is chosen in advance and never open ended. Magpie never touches comp money, and there is no per creator cash fee added on top of a standard comped visit.

What a comp does not include matters just as much. Comped visits are native share only, which means the creator posts to their own audience and you can reshare it in the normal way, but the price does not include a license to run the post as your own ad. If you need ad use, that belongs in the paid lane, where one standard license for 90 days of repost and ad use is included in the booking price. Keeping the lanes separate keeps the cheap lane cheap.

Lane two · Paid posts

When does a paid post make sense, and what is included?

A paid booking makes sense when you want a specific creator, a specific date or a bigger local name, for example an opening, a launch or a seasonal push. The creator sets their own rate, and the floor is $50. Guidance bands are roughly $25 to $75 for the smallest paid posts, $75 to $250 for Locals with 2,000 to 15,000 followers, and $250 to $750 for Headliners with 15,000 or more, moved by local reach and reliability. A creator with a smaller count but a high share of followers in your market can be worth more to you than a larger account spread across the country, and the profile shows local reach before you choose.

The paid price includes more than the post. It includes the same standard deliverable, 1 post or reel plus 1 story, plus one standard license for 90 days of repost and ad use. The fee structure is plain: 6 percent total, split evenly between creator and business, so the creator keeps 97 percent of their rate. Payment is handled through the booking, which protects both sides, and reliability counts here too. Creators who post on time earn Momentum and move up the list businesses browse, so a strong record is part of what you are buying.

You do not need paid posts to start. Most businesses start with comps, learn which creators their customers respond to, and then book paid posts selectively with the creators who already proved they can deliver. That order keeps your first month inexpensive and your later spend informed by real results instead of guesses.

Lane three · Plans

What does a founding plan cover, and what does it not cover?

A founding plan is for owners who want visits as a habit, not a one off project. The published founding pricing is $199 a month for 5 creator visits, $349 a month for 10 visits, or $699 a month for 20 visits, with $100 off the first month and a 3 month minimum. Unused visits roll into the next month and then expire, which encourages a steady rhythm without punishing a slow week. You can see the same numbers any time on the pricing page, because founding pricing is published on the site rather than hidden behind a sales call.

The plan organizes the work: finding creators in your market, ranking them by local reach, setting the written deliverable, tracking the single use in app code at the register, and counting what came back. If you connect Square, creator codes can read back as real register totals, so results are counted rather than guessed. The plan does not include the comp itself. You still provide the meal, service or experience at your cost, and you still choose any paid posts separately and only when you want them.

The Visit Guarantee sits underneath the plan. If a visit produces no post inside a week, that visit does not count against your plan and is replaced in credits, not cash. That rule matters for budgeting, because it means a paid visit slot is tied to a delivered post, not just a visit that happened. A founder reading a budget needs that kind of clarity, and it is why the plan price and the comp cost are always shown separately.

First month math

What does a realistic first month look like at each level?

At the comp only level, your cash cost for creator fees is $0. Your real cost is the product cost of the visits you approve, at ceilings you set. A cautious first month might be three to five visits, each with a written deliverable, each tracked with a code, so you can see posts, content and register activity before you spend anything more. This is the right starting point for an owner who wants proof before a plan.

At the plan level, add the plan price to that same product cost. On the $199 plan, a first month with five visits is $199 in plan cost, minus the $100 first month discount where it applies, plus the product cost of five comps you chose. On the $349 plan, the same logic holds for ten visits, which suits a business that wants a steady weekly rhythm across two or three creators at a time. On the $699 plan, twenty visits supports a launch month or several locations, with more coordination included. In every case, paid posts are optional extras you add only for a specific creator or date.

The mistake to avoid is comparing a plan price to a single creator rate and calling one cheaper. They buy different things. A rate buys one post from one person. A plan buys a repeatable system of visits, ranked by local reach, with counting and a guarantee. Decide how many real visits you want in the next thirty days, price the product cost honestly, and the right level usually names itself.
