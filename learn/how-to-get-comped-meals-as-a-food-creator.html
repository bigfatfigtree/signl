End to end

How does a comped meal work, from start to finish?

It runs in five steps, all inside the app. First, you apply to Magpie free and build your profile: joining starts at 500 total followers across your platforms, and every application gets a decision or a clear status within 2 business days. Second, once you are approved and your market is open, businesses post offers and you accept one you actually want. Third, you visit like a normal guest and redeem the visit in the app, which starts your posting clock. Fourth, you eat, take your photos and notes, and enjoy the meal. Fifth, you post, with the goal of posting within 48 hours of the visit, and keep the post live for 30 or more days.

The deliverable is the Standard Visit: 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post about your real experience. The offer states how many people the comp covers, usually 1 or 2, so read it before you accept and plan who is coming. At the table

What do restaurants expect from you?

Restaurants expect a normal, considerate guest who treats the visit as work as well as dinner. Arrive at the time you booked. Order inside what the offer covers, and if you want extras beyond the comp, expect to pay for those yourself. Check the offer before you go for anything unclear: how many people are covered, what is included, and whether there is a spending cap. If something is still unclear, ask in the app before you visit, not at the table.

During the meal, do the job properly: try what the kitchen is known for, note names and details while they are fresh, and take photos that do not disturb other guests. Be honest with staff if something is wrong, the way any guest would, and give the kitchen a fair chance to fix it. Afterward, post on time. Restaurants remember the creators who were easy to host and quick to deliver, and those creators get invited back. The FTC rule

What does the FTC require when a meal is free?

United States FTC rules are plain on this: a free meal given in exchange for a post is a material connection between you and the restaurant, and a material connection must be disclosed clearly, where people will see it before they judge your recommendation. It does not matter that your opinion is honest, that the meal was small, or that you would have paid for it yourself. If you got it free because you post, you disclose it, every time, on every post and every story.

Do it like this. Put #ad or the Paid Partnership label at the START of your post or caption, in the first line, before any other text or hashtags, in words a normal person understands: for example, #ad: this meal was free in exchange for my honest post. Stories need the disclosure on the story itself, as visible text on that story, because a caption on a separate feed post does not travel with it. Every piece stands alone, so disclose on each one.

And be clear about what is NOT enough, because this is where creators get caught. Writing comped, gifted, or thanks for having me on its own is not sufficient disclosure: those words can mean many things and do not clearly tell a follower that the meal was free in exchange for the post. Burying #ad at the end of a long hashtag string is not enough either. Disclosure goes first, in plain language, or it does not count. Honesty

Do you have to say the food was great?

No, and you must never trade a good review for the meal. Your obligation is an honest post, not a positive one. If the pasta was excellent and the service was slow, say both. If a dish missed, you can say so fairly, the way you would warn a friend, and you can also keep the focus on what you genuinely enjoyed. What you cannot do is promise praise in advance, let the restaurant approve your opinion, or soften a real problem because dinner was free.

On Magpie, no business can require a positive post, and a business that pressures a creator is removed from the platform. That protects you as much as it protects your followers: your credibility is the reason restaurants want you, and one scripted rave can burn it. Post the meal you actually had, in your own voice, and let the disclosure at the top do its job. Readers respect that combination, and so do good restaurants.

The post itself

What should your comped meal post include?

Start with the disclosure, then make the post useful. Name the place and its neighborhood, show the dishes you ordered, say what stood out and who the place suits: a date night, a family dinner, a cheap and cheerful lunch. Honest specifics help a follower decide in seconds, and they help the restaurant too, because the right guests book, not just more guests. Add your stories alongside the post, each with its own disclosure on the story itself.

Then finish the job properly: publish inside the 48-hour goal, keep the post live for 30 or more days, and let your record build. Each on-time post earns Momentum points, +20 per on-time visit, and Momentum orders the list businesses see, so a reliable food creator gets offered the next table sooner. Creator FAQ

What else do creators ask about this?

Is a free meal really an ad under FTC rules?

Yes. A meal provided in exchange for a post is a material connection, and the FTC requires you to disclose material connections clearly. It does not matter that your opinion is honest or that you received no cash. Put #ad or the Paid Partnership label at the start of the post so followers see it before your recommendation.

Is writing comped or thanks for having me enough disclosure?

No. Comped, gifted, or thanks for having me on its own is not sufficient disclosure, because those words do not clearly tell a follower the meal was free in exchange for a post. Use #ad or Paid Partnership at the start of the caption, in plain language, and put the disclosure on each story itself as well.

Where does the disclosure go on stories?

On the story itself, as visible text on that story, at the start. A disclosure in a separate feed caption does not cover your stories, because each piece of content stands alone. Every post and every story about a comped visit needs its own clear disclosure that a follower sees right away.

How many people does a comped meal cover?

The offer states it before you accept: comped meals on Magpie usually cover 1 or 2 people. Read the offer for what is included, who is covered and any spending cap, and ask in the app before you visit if anything is unclear. Anything you order beyond the comp is yours to pay for.

What do I have to post after a comped meal, and how fast?

A Standard Visit: 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, with the goal of posting honestly within 48 hours of the visit, and a week as the outside limit, with #ad or Paid Partnership disclosure at the start, and the post kept live for 30 or more days. You never have to post a positive review: honest only, in your own voice, every time.
