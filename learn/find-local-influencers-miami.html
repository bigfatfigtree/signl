How to find local influencers in Miami

To find local influencers in Miami, search by audience location, not follower count: work the neighborhood location tags in Wynwood, Brickell, Little Havana, Coral Gables and South Beach, check whether the creator's audience posts back in Spanish, English or both, and offer a comp at your cost for an honest post, with the goal of posting within 48 hours of the visit. What does local actually mean in Miami?

Miami has two audiences: the visitors on the beach, and the neighbors who fill your dining room the other fifty weeks of the year. Local means the second one, with proof, and in Miami it also means language.

Start by drawing your real trade area. A Wynwood restaurant pulls a different crowd than a Coral Gables staple or a Doral family spot, and a creator who is perfect for one can be wrong for the others at the same follower count. Miami loyalty is neighborhood loyalty stretched along the causeways: judge creators by where their audience actually lives inside that trade area.

Then check the language of the audience. Two of three Miami-Dade residents speak Spanish at home (US Census), and a creator whose comments run in Spanish is reaching a Miami the English-only search never sees. On Magpie this is the headline number, called local reach: the share of a creator's audience that actually lives here. Miami profiles can also show the share of the audience that speaks Spanish, right next to local reach, because Magpie runs Miami as its Spanish-first founding market. Plan by tier as well as place: Neighbors at 500-2k followers for everyday word of mouth, Locals at 2k-15k for regular local posters, and Headliners at 15k+ for launches.

Where to look

Where do Miami creators actually post?

They cluster around three engines: neighborhood tags, a bilingual scene that posts in Spanish, English and Spanglish, and campuses that reset the audience every fall.

/ 01

Location tags by neighborhood

Search Instagram and TikTok location tags for Wynwood, Brickell, Little Havana, Coral Gables, Coconut Grove, the Design District, Doral and South Beach first. Save accounts that post local food, fitness, beauty and family content repeatedly, in ordinary weeks, not just during Art Basel or a boat show weekend.

/ 02

The bilingual scene

Miami's creator scene runs in two languages at once, and the language of the comments tells you which Miami a creator actually reaches. Read the replies: Spanish-language comment threads from people naming local streets and dishes are the strongest local signal in this market. A creator posting in Spanish to Hialeah and Doral regulars is not a niche pick; for many Miami businesses they are the main channel.

/ 03

Campus creators and your own regulars

University of Miami, Florida International University and Miami Dade College creators reach students and young alumni in exactly the neighborhoods around their campuses. Then look closer to home: your tagged customers, your reviewers, and the neighborhood accounts your regulars already trust. A trusted regular with 900 followers can start the group chat that fills your Saturday.

Use a 10 minute vetting pass: open the profile, check the last 12 posts for Miami places, read comments for local intent and local language, and confirm the creator posts in their own voice. If every post is a brand deal for a shipped product, they are a product creator who lives in Miami, not a local creator Miami follows for local choices.

The offer

What should you offer a Miami creator?

Offer a comp at your own cost, a clear visit window, and freedom to post honestly. That combination gets accepted and gets posted on time.

On Magpie, businesses comp local creators a meal, service or experience at the business cost. The creator posts honestly, with the goal of posting within 48 hours of the visit, with FTC disclosure. The Standard Visit is 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, and delivery is tracked to the post. A post inside a week still counts as delivered; the goal is to post within 48 hours of the visit. For a Wynwood opening or a new Little Havana menu, that structure gives you multiple local voices in the same week without scripting any of them.

If you book paid posts instead, creators set their own rate, paid bookings start at a $50 floor, and the platform fee is 6% total, split evenly, so the creator keeps 97% of their rate. A paid booking includes one standard license: 90 days of repost and ad use, inside the booking terms. State clearly whether the booking is comp only or paid, and never trade a comp for a promised positive review.
