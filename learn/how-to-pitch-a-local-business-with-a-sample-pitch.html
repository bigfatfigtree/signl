How to pitch a local business as a small creator (with a sample pitch)

Most creator pitches fail because they talk about the creator. Good ones talk about one visit, one post and one easy yes. Below are three pitches you can copy and adapt, a restaurant pitch, a salon pitch and a short DM version, each followed by a breakdown of why every line is there.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 15, 2026 · Magpie Learn hub

Claim your founding spot Pitching local businesses
The Short Answer

Pitch one specific visit, not yourself in general. Name who you are, where your audience lives, what you propose to visit and post, and one easy next step. Keep it short, lead with local reach, propose a comp first, never promise a positive review, and follow up once, politely.

Key Takeaways
  • A working pitch has four parts: who you are, where your audience lives, one specific visit with a clear deliverable, and one easy next step.
  • Lead with local reach, propose a comped visit first, and name the deliverable in plain terms a busy owner can judge in seconds.
  • Never promise a positive review, sales, or a follower jump. Honest posts are the product, and overpromising kills the reply.
Before the samples

What makes a pitch work before you copy any sample?

Every sample below uses the same skeleton, and the skeleton matters more than the wording. Part one: who you are and where you post, in one line. Part two: where your audience lives, led by local reach rather than follower count, because a local business can only sell to people who can reach its door. Part three: one specific visit with a named deliverable, usually a Standard Visit on Magpie, 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, posted within 48 hours as the goal, disclosed, and kept live 30 or more days. Part four: one easy next step, a question the owner can answer in one word.

This page is the scripts companion to how to pitch local businesses as a small creator, which covers targeting and timing in depth. Here you get the words themselves. Adapt the details, your city, your topic, your real local share, but keep the structure and keep it honest. Never copy a local share you do not have. Restaurant pitch

What does a full sample pitch to a restaurant look like?

Use this for email or a contact form. Replace every bracket with your real detail, and keep the total short.

Sample pitch, restaurant: Subject: A local post about your brunch, from a neighbor who already eats here. Hi, I am Maya and I post about food in Austin as @yourhandle. About 1,400 people follow me across my platforms and most of them live here in Austin, many in this neighborhood. I come in for brunch most months and I would love to make it official: dinner or brunch for two, comped, and I will post one reel plus a story that shares it about my real experience within 48 hours, disclosed as comped and kept live. If it goes well, I am glad to talk about a paid post for your next menu launch. Would a weekday evening work on your side? Either way, thanks for feeding our neighborhood so well.

Restaurant breakdown

Why does each line of the restaurant pitch work?

Line by line: the subject promises a post, not a meeting, and says neighbor before creator. The first line names a real habit, eating there already, which separates you from every mass pitch the owner deletes. The audience line leads with where followers live and gives a stacked total across platforms, not a single vanity number. The offer is one specific visit with a named deliverable and a timeline, so the owner can price the risk in seconds: a comped meal at her own cost, not retail, in exchange for a defined post. The paid line is soft and future facing, which signals you are building a relationship, not grabbing cash. The close is one yes or no question about a weekday, the slot restaurants most want to fill. Nothing in the pitch promises a glowing review or a line out the door, and that restraint is why it gets believed.

Salon pitch

What does a sample pitch to a salon look like?

Services pitch slightly differently, because the result is visible and lasts for weeks. Lean on the before and after, and on your audience being local women, men or clients who book services near home.

Sample pitch, salon: Hi, I am Jordan and I post about beauty and everyday style in Austin as @yourhandle. My audience is about 900 people across platforms and most of them live here, within a short drive of your salon. I would love to book a cut and color as a comped visit and post a before and after reel, plus a story that shares it, within 48 hours of the appointment, disclosed as comped and kept live. I will show the real process and my honest reaction, including how it wears over the first week in a follow-up story. Would you be open to a weekday slot that is hard to fill? Thanks for making this neighborhood look good.

Salon breakdown and DM

Why does the salon pitch work, and what is the short DM version?

The salon pitch works because it sells what a salon actually needs: local clients who book repeatedly, not one-time visitors. It names distance, a short drive, because services are chosen by convenience. It offers the strongest service format there is, a before and after, and it adds a follow-up story about how the result wears, which answers the question every salon client asks. It also asks for a hard to fill weekday slot, framing your visit as useful inventory, not a favor pulled from a busy Saturday.

For Instagram or TikTok DMs, compress the same skeleton to four lines.

Short DM version: Hi, I post about food and nights out here in Austin as @yourhandle, and most of my followers live here too. I would love to visit as a comped guest and post a reel plus a story about my real experience within 48 hours, disclosed as comped. Open to a quiet weekday? Happy to send my Magpie profile so you can see my local reach.

Follow-up and never promise

How do you follow up, and what should you never promise?

Wait about a week, then send one short follow-up that adds value: a link to a recent local post, a note about a new menu or service you noticed, or a simple no pressure close. Then stop. Owners are busy, inboxes are deep, and a graceful exit keeps the door open for the next season. If you get a no, thank them warmly. Neighborhoods are small and memories are long.

Never promise a positive review, because honest posts are the product and a promised rave is worthless to everyone. Never promise sales, reservations or a follower jump, because you do not control any of them. Never invent a local share, a rate another business paid, or a deadline that does not exist. Let the record talk, keep the pitch human, and make yes feel safe.

Straight Answers

Quick answers on pitching local businesses

How long should a creator pitch be?

Short enough to read in under a minute. A full email pitch can run about 120 to 180 words. A DM pitch should be three or four lines. Owners read pitches between customers, so every extra paragraph lowers your reply rate.

Should my first pitch ask for a comp or for payment?

Ask for a comped visit first in most cases. It costs the business $0 in cash on Magpie, so it is the lowest risk yes. Deliver it well, and a paid booking at your own listed rate becomes a natural second conversation.

How many times should I follow up on a pitch?

Once, politely, after about a week. One short follow-up that adds something useful, a link to a recent local post, is professional. More than one follow-up turns a good pitch into noise, so after that, move on gracefully.
