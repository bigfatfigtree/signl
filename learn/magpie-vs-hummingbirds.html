Magpie vs Hummingbirds

Both platforms match brands with everyday local creators. Almost everything else about them is different: who they are built for, how the work gets done, what it costs and what you own afterward. Here is the honest side by side, taken from their own pages and ours.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 15, 2026 · Magpie Learn hub

The Short Answer

Hummingbirds and Magpie both match brands with local creators, but they are built for different buyers. Hummingbirds runs fully managed campaigns for consumer product brands selling in retail stores, on annual subscriptions. Magpie is built for local businesses people visit in person, with published monthly pricing and local reach shown before you choose.

Key Takeaways
  • Hummingbirds is a fully managed campaign service for consumer product brands in retail stores; Magpie is a roster for local businesses people visit in person.
  • Hummingbirds sells annual subscriptions through a sales team and publishes no price list; Magpie publishes $199, $349 and $699 a month.
  • Hummingbirds content comes with usage rights in perpetuity; on Magpie, paid bookings carry one 90-day license and creators post in their own voice.
The other platform

What is Hummingbirds today?

Hummingbirds is a Des Moines based platform that matches brands with everyday local creators. It started with local businesses and community campaigns, and its site today leads with a different buyer: consumer product brands selling through retail stores. Its CPG pages talk about shelf level awareness at retailers like Target, Costco, Whole Foods, Walmart and Kroger, and its customer stories are product brands: OLLY, Banza, Milton's Craft Bakers and Slate Milk.

The service is fully managed. A dedicated customer success manager and a campaign team handle brief creation, creator selection, logistics and reporting, and onboarding starts with a sales call and an annual agreement. The creators are everyday people: Hummingbirds says its community is 90 percent women, most with 500 to 5,000 followers, and half of them parents. Creators join free, mark the campaigns they are interested in, and the brand or the Hummingbirds team selects who works.

Compensation on Hummingbirds is largely in kind: gift cards, free product and complimentary services, with trade reporting describing a small payment, typically about $50 to $100, when a selected creator submits content. The content itself is a headline feature: brands get rights-ready UGC they can repurpose across their own channels in perpetuity.

The honest part

Where Hummingbirds is the better fit

If you are a product brand trying to prove yourself on retail shelves, Hummingbirds is genuinely built for you and Magpie is not. It has the retailer relationships, the managed team and the case studies in exactly that job, and its creators are practiced at the store find format that moves product. A single location business does not need that machinery, and would be paying for a national campaign team to solve a neighborhood problem.

The reverse is also true. If your customers walk through your door, what you need is a roster of nearby creators whose audiences live near you, chosen on data you can see, at a price you can read before anyone books a sales call. How Magpie works

What working with Magpie looks like instead

You browse a roster built for your city. Every creator profile shows local reach two ways, as a percentage and as an absolute count of local followers, so a creator with 2,000 followers who are mostly your neighbors outranks a creator with 60,000 followers spread across the country. You offer a comped experience, which earns 1 post and 1 story, or you book the creator at the rate on their profile. On paid bookings the creator keeps 97 percent; the 3 percent difference is card processing, and the booking includes one 90-day license to reuse the post.

Magpie is forming founding rosters now. Its core markets are Hampton Roads, Nashville, Austin, Miami, Toronto, Los Angeles and New York City. Anyone in any city can sign up, and bookings attach to each market as it launches, so joining early puts your business at the front of the line when your city opens.

Straight Answers

Magpie vs Hummingbirds, in short

What is the difference between Magpie and Hummingbirds?

Both match brands with local creators. Hummingbirds runs fully managed campaigns for consumer product brands selling in retail stores, on annual subscriptions sold through a sales team, and its content comes with usage rights in perpetuity. Magpie is built for local businesses people visit in person: you choose creators from your city roster by the local reach shown on each profile, visits are comped or booked at the creator's own rate, and pricing is published at $199, $349 and $699 a month.

How much does Hummingbirds cost?

Hummingbirds does not publish a price list. It sells annual subscriptions through its sales team, and Inc. reported in June 2026 that packages typically start around $15,000 to $18,000 a year, with campaign credits on top. Magpie publishes its pricing on this website: $199 a month, $349 a month or $699 a month at founding rates.

Can a restaurant or salon use Hummingbirds?

Hummingbirds began with local businesses, but the buyer its pages describe today is a consumer product brand selling through retail stores, and its campaigns are built around shelf level awareness. A restaurant or salon looking for creators whose audiences live nearby is the business Magpie is built for: restaurants and bars, salons and barbershops, fitness studios, retail and local services, in the markets listed on this page.
