Is a comp enough, or do I owe cash too?

By Jeff, Founder, SIGNL · October 30, 2026

A comp is enough when that is the deal both sides agreed to. On SIGNL an offer is one lane or the other: a comped visit, where the meal, service or experience is the pay, or a paid booking at the creator's own listed rate, starting at a $50 floor, of which the creator keeps 97 percent. The two lanes never combine on one visit, so nobody ever owes cash on top of a comp, or a free meal on top of a paid booking.

What each lane costs What a comped visit is
The short answer

One visit, one lane. The business picks the lane when it posts the offer; the creator picks whether to claim it. A comp agreed as a comp is a complete deal. Cash is never a tip you owe afterward.

The two lanes

The same visit, two ways to pay for it

Both lanes deliver the same Standard Visit: 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, posted to a 48-hour standard, honest and disclosed. Only the payment differs.

/ Lane one

The comped visit

The business covers a meal, service or experience at its own cost, with a stated ceiling, and that coverage is the creator's pay. No cash changes hands and there is no fee on the comp. The business can share the delivered post natively, inside Instagram, with the creator credited, and nothing more.

/ Lane two

The paid booking

The creator is booked at the rate they list on their own profile, from a $50 floor, and keeps 97 percent of it. The business pays when it books, the money is held until the post is live, and the booking includes one standard license: 90 days to repost the post and run it as an ad.

/ Separate

Licensing, after the fact

If a business wants ad rights to a comped post, that is a third, separate transaction: a license request at the creator's listed licensing fee, 90 days, the creator approves each use, and the creator keeps 97 percent of the fee. It never gets bundled into the original visit.

Why the rule exists

Why never combine the two?

Because a mixed deal is where every dispute in this industry is born. "We comped you and slipped you a little cash" leaves both sides unsure what was promised, what the disclosure should say, and what the post was actually worth.

Guides written for owners contradict each other on this question: one calls a product exchange the standard deal, the next insists a comped meal plus a fee is standard. Both are describing the same muddle. A clean lane system ends the argument. The business states the lane in the offer, in the open, before anyone visits. The creator sees the full terms before claiming. If the lane is comp, the comp is the whole pay, and a creator who only wants paid work can simply not claim it. If the lane is paid, the rate is the creator's own number, untouched by the business.

There is one more quiet benefit: disclosure stays simple. A comped post discloses a comp. A paid post discloses a paid booking. A post that was somehow both discloses confusion, and confused disclosures are how businesses end up sharing FTC liability for a post they did not write. The disclosure rules themselves are in FTC disclosure for comped posts.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Can I offer a comp plus a small cash fee on one visit?

Not on SIGNL. The two lanes never combine on a single visit, by rule. A mixed offer muddles everything downstream: what the creator discloses, what the post is worth, and what each side thinks was promised. Post the comp, or post the paid booking. Both are always available.

Who sets the paid rate?

The creator. Every creator lists their own paid rate on their profile, starting at a $50 floor, and keeps 97 percent of it when a business books them at that rate. The business chooses the lane when it posts the offer, and the creator chooses whether to claim it. Neither side negotiates the other down.

Does a paid booking include ad rights?

Yes. A paid booking includes one standard license: 90 days for the business to repost the post and run it as an ad, inside the booking terms. A comped post is different: the business can share it natively, inside Instagram, with the creator credited, and nothing more. Licensing a comped post afterward is a separate request at the creator's listed fee.

When should a business choose the paid lane?

When it needs a specific creator, a specific date, or a bigger local name: a launch night, a grand opening, a Headliner whose audience the business wants on a set day. Comped visits cover the steady rhythm of local posts; paid bookings buy precision.

When should a creator take a comp instead of holding out for paid?

Early, or for a place you genuinely love. A comped visit builds the delivery record that businesses sort by, and one strong comped visit regularly turns into a paid regular gig. The honest test is the one on the charge page: price your time, and if the trade still feels good, take it with a clear head.
