How much should a small creator charge?

What should you charge by tier?

Start with your tier, because tier is the range a business expects. On SIGNL your tier is automatic, set by your total audience stacked across platforms, and the paid guidance per post is simple: Neighbors charge $25-75, Locals charge $75-250, and Headliners charge $250-750. Those are guidance ranges for a paid post booked at your own listed rate, not a salary scale, and you set the exact number inside your range.

One rule sits under the table: the floor for a listed paid rate on SIGNL is $50. So a Neighbors creator pricing a listed paid booking starts at $50, even though small paid posts in the wider market can go lower. Below a paid booking, comped visits are separate: the meal or service is the pay, and comp-only is always fine forever. Inside the range

What moves you up or down inside your range?

Four things move you inside your range, and follower count is only the frame, not the mover. First, local audience share: a Locals creator whose followers mostly live in the business's city is worth the top of the range to that business, because nearly every view is a possible customer. Second, engagement that shows intent: comments asking where a place is, saves, shares and replies beat a pile of silent likes.

Third, reliability. A creator who has posted on time, every time, within the 48-hour standard is a safe booking, and safe bookings get paid better than gambles. Your Momentum record makes that visible to businesses. Fourth, niche fit: a creator who posts only about coffee, or only about family days out, is worth more to a business in that niche than a generalist with the same count, because their audience already buys that thing. Price yourself honestly on those four, and your number will be easy to defend.

Comp versus cash

How does a comp compare with cash, honestly?

Do the math from both sides before you decide a comp is too small or a rate is too high. Say a restaurant comps a dinner with a $60 menu value. That dinner does not cost the restaurant $60: it costs their product cost, the ingredients and the kitchen time, which is well under the menu price, especially on a quiet night when the table would sit empty. That is why comps are easy for businesses to offer, and why they are a good first deal for both sides.

Now your side. Price your time honestly: travel, the visit itself, shooting and editing, writing the caption and posting. If that adds up to three hours, a $60 menu value dinner is not $60 of pay, it is a nice dinner plus proof, a relationship and a track record entry. Sometimes that trade is worth it, early on or for a place you love. Sometimes it is not, and then the honest move is to set a paid rate or pass politely. What never works is counting comp value as cash income. Keep the two ledgers separate, like the creators page does, and read how to get comped meals as a food creator before your first comped visit.

The fee

What does SIGNL take from a paid booking?

Almost nothing, and it is in the open. Joining, listing and comped visits are free for creators. On a paid booking, the only fee is 6 percent total, split evenly between you and the business, so you keep 97 percent of your rate. Set a $100 rate and you keep $97. Set a $200 rate and you keep $194. There is no second cut hiding behind it.

Payment is held by SIGNL and released when your post is live, which protects both sides: the business knows the post is coming, and you know the money is already there before you visit. That structure is also why your rate can be simple. You do not need to pad it for platform fees, chasing invoices or the risk of not being paid. The full fee picture is also covered in the creator FAQ.

Raising rates

When should you raise your rate?

Raise your rate when your proof changes, not when your mood does. Good triggers: you cross into the next tier, your local share climbs, a business books you a second time, or you have a clean run of on-time posts that a business can see in your record. Raise in small steps inside your range, $25 at a time is plenty, and let each new rate hold for a few bookings before you move again.

Do not raise your rate in the middle of an agreed booking, and do not apologize for a fair one. A rate backed by local reach, engagement and reliability is not a favor you are asking for. It is a price for a result a business can check. If a business wants comp-only instead, that is a legitimate choice on both sides: comp-only is always fine forever on SIGNL, and many creators mix comps for places they love with paid posts for repeat work.

Red flags

What pricing red flags should you walk away from?

Three patterns should end a conversation. First, pay-to-play: any platform or business that charges you to join, to list or to get booked has the model backwards. On SIGNL, businesses pay the platform and creators never pay. Second, a perpetual rights grab: a business asking to use your content forever, in any ad, for no extra pay. On SIGNL, usage rights come with paid bookings for a set window, never in perpetuity. If someone wants your post in ads forever, that is a separate, paid negotiation.

Third, strings that break honesty: a positive review required in advance, a script you must read as if it were your opinion, or payment that only arrives if the post performs. A good business is buying your real reach and your real voice. Creator FAQ

What else do creators ask about this?

What is the $50 floor on SIGNL paid rates?

The floor is the lowest rate you can list for a paid booking on SIGNL: $50 per post. The Neighbors guidance range starts at $25 for small paid posts generally, but a rate listed on SIGNL starts at $50. Comped visits sit outside this entirely, because on a comped visit the meal or service is the compensation.

How much of my rate do I actually keep?

You keep 97 percent of your rate. The only fee on a paid booking is 6 percent total, split evenly between creator and business, so the creator side is 3 percent. A $100 rate pays you $97, and a $200 rate pays you $194. Payment is held by SIGNL and released when your post is live.

Should I charge cash for a comped visit?

No. A comped visit and a paid booking are two different deals. On a Standard Visit the comp itself is the pay: 1 post or reel plus 2 stories in exchange for the meal or service. If a business wants to book you at your paid rate instead, that is a paid booking, priced from your tier guidance. Comp-only is always fine forever.

When can I set a paid rate?

Any creator can set a paid rate on their profile, at any tier. Use your tier guidance to place it: Neighbors $25-75 per post, Locals $75-250, Headliners $250-750, with listed rates starting at the $50 floor. Where you land inside the range depends on your local share, engagement, reliability and niche fit.

Does SIGNL charge creators to join or get booked?

No. SIGNL is free for creators. Always. Businesses pay the platform. You never pay to join, to list, or to get booked. If any platform asks you to pay for access to brand deals, treat that as a red flag and walk away.
