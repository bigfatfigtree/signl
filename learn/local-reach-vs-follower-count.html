Local reach vs. follower count: which one gets you booked?

Follower count is the number everyone quotes, and for a local business it is the wrong number to lead with. Local reach is the share of your audience that lives close enough to act. This page puts the two head to head, with the simple math and worked examples, for creators and for the businesses deciding who to book.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 15, 2026 · Magpie Learn hub

The Short Answer

Local reach gets you booked more often than follower count. A business can only sell to people who can reach its door, so 1,000 followers who mostly live nearby beat 20,000 scattered followers. Magpie ranks creators by local reach first, because neighbors who can walk in are what businesses buy.

Key Takeaways
  • Local reach is the share of your audience that lives in the business market, and followers times local share equals neighbors who can walk in.
  • A smaller, mostly local audience can deliver more real customers to one business than a much larger scattered audience.
  • Magpie shows local reach on a creator profile before a business chooses and ranks rosters by it, never by follower count alone.
The two numbers

What do follower count and local reach each actually measure?

Follower count measures size. It is every account that follows you, everywhere, including people who followed for one viral post in another state or another country and will never be near your city. Local reach measures fit. It is the share of that audience that lives in one specific market, the city or metro where a business operates. A creator can have a modest count and very high local reach, or a huge count and almost no local reach in the market a business cares about. Those are completely different buys, and treating them as the same is how local marketing budgets get wasted.

Magpie defines local reach plainly: the share of a creator audience that lives in the business market. It is shown on the creator profile before a business chooses, alongside the absolute local audience a creator can point to in that market. For the definition on its own, read what is local reach. This page is the comparison: when the two numbers point in different directions, which one should decide the booking? For a local business, the answer is local reach, and the math below shows why. New York creators can see the idea at city scale on the New York City page, where a metro is really many neighborhoods stacked together.

The math

What is the math that turns followers into neighbors?

The equation is simple: followers multiplied by local share equals neighbors who can walk in. Here is a worked example with clearly hypothetical round numbers, labeled as an example and not a measurement of any real account. Example one: a creator has 2,000 followers and 60 percent of them live in the business city. That is about 1,200 neighbors who can visit. Example two: a creator has 20,000 followers and 3 percent of them live in that same city. That is about 600 neighbors. The second creator has ten times the follower count and half the local audience. A business that books on count alone pays for the bigger headline and gets the smaller room.

Run the example the other way and it still holds. Example three: two creators each have 5,000 followers. One is 80 percent local, about 4,000 neighbors. The other is 10 percent local, about 500 neighbors. Same count, same tier, eight times the local value. This is why Magpie ranks rosters by local reach and never by follower count alone, and why a business browsing Magpie sees local reach on the profile up front instead of discovering it after the post underperforms.

For creators

What does this mean if you are a creator?

It means your most valuable growth is local growth. A hundred new followers in your city can be worth more to your deal flow than a thousand new followers scattered everywhere, because they raise both your count and your local share at once. Grow where you live: post about your neighborhood, engage with local accounts, show up in local conversations, and keep your city clear in your bio and on your Magpie profile. Connect every platform you post on, because your tier uses your stacked total across platforms, and a platform where your audience is strongly local can lift your whole picture.

It also means never buy followers. Bought followers are almost never local, they do not engage, and they dilute the one number that gets you booked. A smaller honest audience with a high local share will out-book a padded audience every time a business can see both numbers, and on Magpie it can. For businesses

What does this mean if you are a business?

It means the cheapest-looking booking can be the most expensive mistake. A large creator with a scattered audience may charge more and deliver fewer neighbors than a small creator whose audience is your neighborhood. Before you book anyone, ask the local question: how many of this creator followers live within reach of my door? On Magpie you do not have to ask and hope for an honest answer, because local reach is on the profile before you choose, and the roster is ranked by it. Founding business plans are public on the site at $199, $349 or $699 a month for 5, 10 or 20 creator visits, so you can also compare cost per visit against the local audience each creator brings.

Follower count still has a job. It sets context and tier, and a creator with both scale and local share, a Headliner whose audience is deeply local, is a powerful booking. The mistake is using count as the only filter. Count tells you how loud a creator is. Local reach tells you how many of the people listening can become your customers this week.

The verdict

So which one gets you booked?

Local reach gets you booked, and follower count gets you considered. A business shortlist may start with tier and topic, but the booking decision, the one that spends money and offers a comp, turns on how much of your audience can actually show up. That is good news for most creators reading this, because local reach is buildable at any size. You do not need to go viral in fifty states. You need to be known, trusted and clearly local in one place.

Straight Answers

Quick answers on local reach and follower count

Which matters more for a local booking, local reach or follower count?

Local reach, for a local business. Follower count tells a business how many people follow you in total. Local reach tells it how many of those people live in its market and can actually visit. A business books the audience that can walk in.

How is local reach calculated in plain terms?

In plain terms, take total followers and multiply by the local share. For example, 2,000 followers with half living locally gives about 1,000 neighbors who can visit. Those example numbers are illustrations, not measurements of any real account.

Does follower count matter at all?

Yes, as context and for tier. On Magpie tiers are set by total followers stacked across platforms, Neighbors at 500 to 2,000, Locals at 2,000 to 15,000, and Headliners at 15,000 and up. But ranking inside the roster runs on local reach, not count alone.
