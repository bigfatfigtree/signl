A seasonal campaign calendar for local businesses

Most local businesses market when they remember to. The ones that win the year market when their customers are already paying attention. A seasonal campaign calendar lines your creator visits up with the moments your area already gathers around, and puts visits into the slow weeks that need a reason to come in.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 3, 2026 · Magpie Learn hub

For businesses See pricing
The short answer

What is a seasonal campaign calendar?

It is a one page year plan. Across the top: the months. Down the side: the moments that move your business. Openings and launches. Holidays and gift weeks. School terms starting and ending. Festivals, sports, and the big local weekends. Then the honest part: the slow weeks, written down instead of discovered the hard way. Every moment gets one job, fill it, launch it, or fix it, and a small set of creator visits booked to do that job.

The calendar works because creator posts land harder when the audience is already in the mood. A post about a new menu reads as news in launch week. The same post in a random week reads as an ad. Timing is the cheapest upgrade in local marketing, and it costs nothing but planning. If you are new to the terms, the local creator marketing glossary defines every word used here.

The moments

Build around the moments people already gather

Peak moments. Holidays, celebration weeks, and the busiest weekends of your year. Demand is already there, so the job is capture: get seen by people choosing where to go. Book more visits than usual, pick creators with the strongest local reach, and make the offer the one you want shared, not the cheapest thing you sell.

Launch moments. A grand opening, a new menu, a new service, a second location, a relaunch. News is the easiest content a creator can post, because it gives their audience something they did not know yesterday. A creator night fits naturally here: one evening, a small group, one clear deliverable each.

Calendar moments. School terms, sports seasons, festival weeks, and the local weekends your customers plan around. These repeat every year, which makes them the backbone of the calendar. Campus creators are the obvious example: term starts are when routines form, and a business booked into that window gets chosen early and kept.

Slow weeks. Every local business has them, and they are where a calendar earns its keep. A slow week with no plan is just a quiet room. A slow week with a specific, time bound reason to visit, posted by creators whose followers live nearby, is a campaign. The Black Friday guide shows the pattern at full intensity: Black Friday marketing for local businesses.

Working backwards

Book the visit before the moment, not during it

Work backwards from the day you want to be busy. Four to six weeks out, pick the moment and the job. Three to four weeks out, choose creators by local reach, the share of their audience that lives in your market, not by follower count. Two weeks out, send the brief: the facts, the must mentions, the date, and the deliverable, with direction instead of a script, as how to brief a creator sets out. The visit happens before the peak, so the post is live while people are still deciding.

The posting standard stays the same in every season. A comped visit on Magpie is 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, posted within 48 hours, honest and disclosed. The full standard is in what a comped creator visit actually includes. Creators who post on time earn Momentum points, and Momentum raises their visibility to local businesses, which is one more reason the reliable creators get claimed first for the big weeks.

Lanes and cost

Match the lane to the moment

Use comped visits for steady presence: the regular rhythm of posts that keeps your business in local feeds month after month. The meal, service, or experience is the compensation, and the cost to you is your product at your cost, not retail. Use paid bookings for the moments that matter most: opening week, a launch you cannot redo, or a creator whose local audience you specifically want. Seasonal FAQ

What else do businesses ask about seasonal campaigns?

What is a seasonal campaign calendar for a local business?

A seasonal campaign calendar is a year plan that matches creator visits to the moments your customers already gather around: openings, new launches, holidays, school terms, local festivals and sports, and the slow weeks that need a reason to visit. Instead of booking creators at random, you book them when attention is already high, or when you most need it.

How far ahead should a local business plan creator visits?

Plan the visit four to six weeks before the moment you want to fill. That leaves time to pick creators by local reach, write a clear brief, agree the visit date, and let the post land while the moment is still in front of people. Big moments like holiday weeks and opening weeks deserve the longer end of that window, because the best local creators get claimed first.

What should a local business promote in its slow weeks?

Promote a reason, not a discount by default. A new menu item, a new service, a weeknight experience, a group offer, or a first look at something coming soon gives a creator something real to post about and gives their audience a reason to visit now instead of later. Slow week posts work best when the offer is specific, time bound, and easy to act on.
