How much should you charge for a sponsored post?

Pricing feels personal, so creators either guess low out of nerves or copy a big creator rate that does not fit a local deal. This guide gives you the actual math: the guidance bands by tier, the three things that move you inside a band, and the two line items most small creators forget to charge for at all.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 15, 2026 · Magpie Learn hub

The Short Answer

Charge by local value, not follower count alone. On Magpie the smallest paid posts guide at roughly $25 to $75, Locals at $75 to $250, and Headliners at $250 to $750. Local reach, reliability, deliverables, usage rights and exclusivity move you inside each band.

Key Takeaways
  • Guidance bands on Magpie run roughly $25 to $75 for the smallest paid posts, $75 to $250 for Locals, and $250 to $750 for Headliners.
  • Local reach, reliability and the deliverable itself move you up or down inside your band more than follower count does.
  • Usage rights and exclusivity are separate line items, never free extras folded silently into a post price.
The bands

What are the guidance bands by tier?

Start with the band, then adjust. On Magpie, tiers are set by your total followers stacked across platforms: Neighbors at 500 to 2,000, Locals at 2,000 to 15,000, and Headliners at 15,000 and up. The guidance for paid posts is roughly $25 to $75 for the smallest paid posts, $75 to $250 for Locals, and $250 to $750 for Headliners. These are guidance bands, not a price list a business must accept, and creators on Magpie set their own rate with a $50 floor. The band keeps you in a realistic range for local deals, where budgets are real and repeat work matters more than one big invoice.

Why are local rates lower than the national numbers you see online? Because a local deal is narrower and more certain. A neighborhood business is buying your nearby audience for one visit, not a national campaign with a usage package. In return you get something national deals rarely offer a small creator: a real chance at monthly repeat work from the same business, plus a public track record that raises your rate over time. The broader background is in how much a small creator should charge, while this page works the per-post math itself. You can also compare markets on the markets page.

Inside the band

What moves you up or down inside your band?

Three things move you. First, local reach. Two creators can sit in the same tier with the same follower count, and the one whose audience mostly lives in the business market is worth more to that business, because more of the post lands on people who can walk in. Magpie shows local reach on your profile before a business chooses and ranks by it, so this is not a claim you make in a pitch, it is a number a business can already see.

Second, reliability. A creator who posts on time, every time, is a safer buy than a creator with a slightly bigger audience and a patchy record. On Magpie, on-time posts earn Momentum points, +20 for an on-time post, and Momentum orders the list businesses browse. Third, the deliverable. A reel that takes an afternoon to shoot and edit is worth more than a quick photo post, a visit that needs travel is worth more than one around the corner, and a post you keep live longer carries more value. Price the work in front of you, not an abstract idea of your account.

  • ✓Deliverable: format, effort, travel and how long the post stays live
  • ✓Track record: past visits a business can read, which turn a guess into a known quantity

Separate line items

Why are usage rights and exclusivity separate line items?

A post price pays for the post: your audience seeing your honest content on your channel. Usage is different. When a business wants to repost your content, run it as an ad, or use it on its own channels, it is buying rights in your work, and that has its own value. On Magpie this is kept simple and fair: a paid booking includes one standard license, 90 days of repost and ad use, in the booking price. A comped visit, by contrast, is native share only, with no usage rights included. Anything beyond the standard license, a longer term, paid ads at scale, or use in print or in store, should be named and priced as its own line item before the visit, not argued about after.

Exclusivity is the other forgotten line item. If a business asks you not to post about its competitors for a period, it is asking you to turn down other income, and that restriction should carry its own price and a clear end date. Keep it narrow: one category, one market, one short period. If a request feels vague or open ended, ask for it in writing with dates. Clear line items protect both sides, and businesses that plan to work with you again will respect a creator who prices calmly and in the open. Your profile itself should carry your rate clearly, which is the point of your creator profile is your rate card.

Comp versus cash

When does a comp beat cash?

Sometimes the meal, service or experience is worth more to you than a small cash fee would be, especially early on. A comped visit costs a business $0 in cash, so it is the easiest yes, and a delivered comp gives you proof, content and a relationship. Take the comp when the experience has real value to you, when the business is one you want a repeat relationship with, or when you need one more clean delivery to justify the top of your band. Remember that comped meals and services count as taxable income at fair market value, so keep a simple record of what you received.

Take cash when the business wants more than a standard visit: extra deliverables, usage beyond the standard license, exclusivity, or a tight deadline. And never run both lanes on one visit. On Magpie a deal is either a comped visit, paid in the experience itself, or a paid booking at your listed rate, never both on the same visit. That clean split is what keeps pricing honest and repeat bookings simple.

Raising your rate

How do you raise your rate without losing the business?

Raise it with evidence, in small steps, at natural moments. After three or four on-time deliveries for the same business, your next booking can carry a higher rate, and you can say why in one calm sentence: your local audience grew, your posts are landing well, and your new rate reflects that. Businesses expect rates to move as a creator proves out. What they dislike is a surprise jump with no record behind it.

Keep your listed rate current on Magpie, because your profile is where businesses decide. A rate that matches your tier band, your local reach and your record reads as professional. A rate copied from a national creator with a different audience reads as a risk. Price like a local business yourself: clear, fair, and easy to say yes to again next month.

Straight Answers

Quick answers on sponsored post pricing

What is a fair first paid rate for a small creator?

Use your tier band as the starting point. The smallest paid posts guide at roughly $25 to $75, Locals at $75 to $250, and Headliners at $250 to $750. Inside the band, strong local reach and a clean delivery record put you toward the top.

Are usage rights included in my post price?

On Magpie a paid booking includes one standard license, 90 days of repost and ad use, in the booking price. Anything beyond that, longer use, wider ad use or use in other channels, is a separate conversation and a separate price.

How much of my rate do I keep on Magpie?

You keep 97 percent of paid bookings. The total fee is 6 percent, split evenly between creator and business, and creators set their own rate with a $50 floor. Magpie is free for creators to join and list.
