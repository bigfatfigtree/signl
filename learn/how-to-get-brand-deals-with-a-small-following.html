How to get brand deals with a small following

Yes, you can get brand deals with a small following, and local deals are the fastest route. Local businesses buy where your audience lives, not how many followers you have, so a local profile, consistent local posts and a clear pitch will beat a big scattered audience.

The short answer

Why do local businesses book small creators?

Local businesses book small creators because their customers live within driving distance of the door. A national brand can use followers anywhere, so it shops by follower count. A restaurant, salon or shop in your city can only use the followers who can actually walk in, so it shops by audience location. That is the whole opportunity: a creator with 900 followers, most of them in one city, can put more real customers in front of a local business than a creator with 90,000 followers scattered across the country.

Trust does the rest. Your followers know you as a neighbor, a regular, a coworker, the person they ask where to eat. When you post about a place, it reads as a recommendation from someone they know, not as an ad. Businesses understand that difference, and it is why SIGNL tiers start at just 500 followers. Your profile

How do you build a profile that proves you are local?

A business decides in about ten seconds whether your audience is local. Make those seconds easy. Put your city in your bio, not just in a location tag on one post. Name your neighborhood on your SIGNL profile. Connect every platform you post on, because your tier is set by your total audience stacked across platforms, and a stacked audience is often much bigger than any single account suggests.

Then keep your local audience share high and honest. Follow and engage with people in your city, post about places near you, and let your audience grow where you live. Do not buy followers: bought followers are almost never local, they drag your local share down, and businesses can see the damage on your profile. A smaller, genuinely local audience beats a big, scattered one every time.

  • ✓City in your bio: your city and what you post about, in one plain line a business can read at a glance.
  • ✓Neighborhood on SIGNL: the specific part of town you live in and post about, not just the metro name.
  • ✓Local share kept high: grow where you live, and never buy followers who will never visit your city.
  • ✓Every platform connected: your tier uses your stacked total, so link each account you actually post on.
Proof first

What should you post before you pitch anyone?

Post about local spots consistently before you ask anyone for anything. Pick the places you already go: the taco truck, the barber, the bakery on your street. Post honest, useful content about them with no deal attached. Two or three local posts a week for a month gives a business something real to judge: your voice, your photos, your comments, and whether people near you respond.

Consistency matters more than polish. A business would rather book a creator who posts every week in a clear, honest style than a creator with one beautiful post and a silent feed. Keep your posts live, reply to comments, and save your best local posts where a business can find them fast. That body of work is your portfolio, and it works harder than any pitch deck at your size. The pitch

What goes in a pitch that gets a reply?

Keep it short, specific and local. Say who you are and where you post. Say where your audience lives, with your local share if you know it. Propose one concrete visit: what you would come in for, what you would post, and when. Name the deliverable plainly, for example a Standard Visit on SIGNL: 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, live within 48 hours, kept live for 30 or more days, with clear disclosure. Close with an easy next step, not a demand.

A pitch you can copy: Hi, I post about food in Norfolk as @yourhandle. About 1,100 people follow me and most of them live here in Hampton Roads. I would love to visit for dinner for two and post a reel, plus a story that shares it, about my real experience within 48 hours, disclosed as comped. If it goes well, I am happy to talk about a paid post next time. Either way, thanks for running a place our neighborhood loves.

Notice what that pitch does not do: it does not lead with a follower count, promise a positive review, or ask for cash on the first contact. It leads with location, proof and a low risk offer. Businesses reply to pitches that make saying yes feel safe.

The on-ramp

How do comped visits turn into paid work?

Treat comped visits as the on-ramp, not the destination. A comped visit costs a business very little to try, so it is the easiest yes you will ever get. Deliver it well: show up when you said you would, post within 48 hours, disclose clearly, and keep the post live. Each delivered visit builds your track record and your Momentum, the points system that orders the list businesses see when they browse creators. On-time posts earn +20 points, so reliability literally moves you up the list.

After two or three clean visits, you have proof a business can read, and that is when paid work starts. Set a paid rate on your profile, using the tier guidance in how much a small creator should charge, and let repeat businesses book you at it. Comp-only is always fine forever if you prefer it, but most creators find the path is the same: comp first, proof second, paid third. Creator FAQ

What else do creators ask about this?

Can I get brand deals with under 500 followers?

Yes. Anyone can apply to SIGNL at any follower count, even under 500. Your tier is set automatically by your total audience across platforms: Neighbors at 500 to 2,000, Locals at 2,000 to 15,000, and Headliners at 15,000 or more. What gets you booked is a local, engaged audience, not a big raw number.

Do local businesses care about follower count or location?

Location first. A local business can only sell to people who can reach its door, so it looks at where your followers live before it looks at how many you have. A small creator whose audience is mostly in one city is more valuable to that city's businesses than a large creator whose audience is spread everywhere.

What should my first pitch include?

Four things: who you are and where you post, where your audience lives, one concrete visit you are proposing with a clear deliverable, and an easy next step. Lead with your local audience share, not your follower count, and never promise a positive review. Honest posts are the product.

Are comped visits real brand deals?

Yes. A comped visit is a deal where the meal, service or experience is the compensation. On SIGNL a Standard Visit is 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, posted honestly within 48 hours with disclosure and kept live for 30 or more days. Comped visits are also the normal on-ramp to paid bookings once you have a track record.
