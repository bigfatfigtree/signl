UGC for local businesses: what it is, what it costs, and why local beats generic

Owners search for UGC when what they really need is fresh, believable content that brings people in. Generic UGC delivers a file from a stranger. Local creator content delivers the same kind of file, plus something generic UGC never includes: a local audience that already follows the person who made it.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 5, 2026 · Magpie Learn hub

For businesses See pricing
The short answer

UGC is the content. Local reach is what makes it work.

UGC stands for user generated content. In plain terms, it is a photo or video made in a real person style instead of a polished ad style. Businesses buy it because it looks like a customer, not a campaign. The definition is in the local creator marketing glossary alongside the other terms owners get sold.

For a local business, the content alone is only half the purchase. Your customers live near you. Content made by someone with no audience near you still needs your own page or your own ad spend to reach anyone local. Content made by a local creator starts in the feed of people who can walk in. That difference is local reach: the share of a creator audience that lives in your market. Magpie ranks creators by that number, not by follower count.

If you are still deciding which purchase you need, start with UGC vs. influencer posts for local businesses. This page goes one step further: once you know you want creator style content, here is what it should cost, what rights should come with it, and why the local version wins.

Generic vs. local

Three gaps generic UGC cannot close

  • ✓No local distribution. A generic UGC creator ships you a file and their work is done. Nobody local has seen it yet. A local creator post is published to a local audience first, so distribution happens before reuse is even discussed.
  • ✓No local trust. Viewers can tell when a creator has never set foot in a place. A local creator visited, experienced it like a real guest, and posted honestly about it. That honesty is the product. A brief gives direction and facts, never a script, as how to brief a creator sets out.
  • ✓No visit behind the content. Generic UGC is often made from a product shipped to a home. A local creator visit happens inside the business: the room, the staff, the actual dish or service. The standard on Magpie is clear, in what a comped creator visit actually includes: 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, posted within 48 hours, honest and disclosed.

Illustrative example, labeled as such: a generic creator with 20,000 followers spread nationally might have only a few dozen followers within driving distance of your door. A local creator with 3,000 followers at 60 percent local reach has about 1,800 followers nearby. The smaller file comes with the bigger usable audience. The math is illustrative, but the direction is the point: for a business people visit in person, concentration beats size.

What it costs

Priced in the open, not by the piece

Magpie does not sell UGC videos by the piece, because a local business does not need a folder of files. It needs visits that produce posts. The cost picture is in how much local influencers cost, and the short version is published here in full.

Founding business plans are Self-Service at $199 a month for up to 5 creator visits, Growth at $349 a month for up to 10, and Managed at $699 a month for up to 20. Founding businesses take $100 off the first month, so the first month is $99, $249, or $599. Plans carry a 3 month minimum, there is no per location charge, and unused visits roll into the next month, then expire. At full use, that is about $40 per visit on Self-Service and about $35 per visit on the upper plans, before the value of the post itself.

Two lanes sit on top of the plan, and they never blend on one visit. A comped visit costs product, not cash: the meal, service, or experience is the compensation. A paid booking costs cash at the creator's own listed rate, with a $50 floor, and the creator keeps 97 percent. Creators who post on time earn Momentum points, and Momentum raises their visibility to local businesses, so the roster you pick from next month is shaped by who delivered this month.

The rights line

What reuse you actually get, visit by visit

This is where generic UGC pitches get vague and local businesses get burned. A file without clear rights is a file you may not be able to run. On Magpie, rights follow the visit type, never the plan tier, and they are stated before the visit.

  • ✓Comped visit: native share only on every plan. You may reshare the post natively with credit. There is no ad use, no download and repost as your own, on any tier.
  • ✓Paid booking: one standard license is included in the booking terms: 90 days of repost and paid ad use. Never perpetuity.
  • ✓Licensing a comped post: send a license request at the creator's listed licensing fee, for 90 days. The creator approves each use, and the creator keeps 97 percent of the fee.

That structure gives a business both things it was searching for, in the right order. First, a local post to a local audience. Second, a clear, priced right to reuse the posts that earned it. Distribution first, reuse second, with the license agreed before any ad runs.

UGC FAQ

What else do businesses ask about UGC?

What is UGC for a local business?

UGC is user generated content: a photo or video made in a creator style for a business to use on its own channels. For a local business, the useful version is narrower. It is a post made by a creator who lives in your market, first published to that creator's own local audience, then saved for the business to reuse only under the rights agreed for that visit.

How much does UGC cost a local business on Magpie?

Magpie does not sell UGC by the piece. A business runs creator visits on a published founding plan: Self-Service is $199 a month for up to 5 creator visits, Growth is $349 a month for up to 10, and Managed is $699 a month for up to 20, with $100 off the first month, a 3 month minimum, and unused visits rolling into the next month, then expiring. A comped visit costs product, not cash. A paid booking costs the creator's own listed rate, with a $50 floor, and the creator keeps 97 percent.

Why do local creator posts beat generic UGC?

Generic UGC gives you a file. A local creator post gives you a file plus an audience that can act on it. The creator's followers live nearby, already follow that person by choice, and can visit this week. A stranger with no local audience can make a clean video, but that video starts with no local trust and no local distribution.

Can a business run a creator post as an ad?

Only with the right license. A comped visit on Magpie is native share only on every plan, with no ad use. A paid booking includes one standard license for 90 days of repost and paid ad use. To run a comped post as an ad, the business sends a license request at the creator's listed licensing fee, for 90 days, the creator approves each use, and the creator keeps 97 percent.
