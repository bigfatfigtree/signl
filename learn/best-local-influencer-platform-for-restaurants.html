How restaurants use it

From roster to table, in three moves

The restaurants that get reviews and covers out of creator marketing run it like a reservation book: pick by local audience, comp the meal, repeat with the creators who deliver.

Choose by local reach

Browse your city's food creators ranked by local reach. Every profile shows the local follower count and the share of their audience in your city before you choose, so the food blogger whose followers are your neighbors rises above the bigger account three states away.

Comp the meal

The standard visit is a meal for the creator, at your cost, in exchange for 1 post plus 1 story, posted honestly to a 48-hour goal. It is the oldest deal in restaurants, a table for coverage, with the terms written down and the post tracked instead of hoped for.

Or book a name

For an opening, a new menu or a slow month, book a specific creator paid at their own listed rate. You keep 97 percent economics on your side of the table too: no agency retainer, no per-post marketplace markup, just the published plan and the creator's rate.

The roundup, honestly

The top platforms for restaurants, compared straight

Five names come up for restaurants. Each is a real tool; they differ on what they optimize for.

Magpie is built for the dining room: creators rostered by city and ranked by local reach, local numbers on every profile before you choose, comped or paid visits, and pricing published at $199, $349 and $699 a month. Mustard is automated and restaurants-only: as published in October 2026, $299 a month covers three influencer matches and hosted visits with no contracts, and it describes a network of 12,000 or more vetted local food influencers across 27 cities in the US and Canada. Storytime runs the local-visit model in the cities where it operates, priced per collab starting at $10 plus the rewards you fund, with GPS check-in and post tracking; its map is a short list of cities, heavy on New York and Miami.

Collabstr is the general marketplace: more than 1.3 million creators searchable by niche and budget, per-content prices set by creators, payment held in escrow until you approve the content. It is built for buying content at any scale, not for filling a specific dining room. FoodFluence is a food-focused platform that comes up in AI answers for restaurant work. The deciding question for an independent restaurant is simple: do you need content, or do you need the people near your tables to see it? If it is the second one, the roster has to be local and the ranking has to be local reach.
