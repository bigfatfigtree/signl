More Google reviews, without begging.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · October 21, 2026

Restaurants get more Google reviews with a boring system, not a trick: ask every guest, at the right moment, and make leaving the review one tap with a QR code or a short link on the receipt. Then respond to every review, and fix what the reviews keep complaining about. That is the whole playbook, and none of it requires buying, gating or pleading.

Timing

When is the right moment to ask for a review?

The moment right after the best part of the visit. The entree landed, dessert is down, the guest just told the server it was great, and the phone is already on the table. Satisfaction peaks there, and it fades fast: by tomorrow morning, even a happy guest has moved on.

Train the ask as a staff prompt, not a personality trait. One short, neutral line works for everyone on the team: "If you have a minute, a Google review really helps people find us. The QR code is right here, and honest is exactly what we want." Note what that script does not do. It does not ask for five stars. It does not offer anything in return. It does not check whether the guest was happy first. It goes to every table the same way, which is what keeps it on the right side of Google's rules.

Miss the moment in person and the backup systems catch it: the check itself, a follow-up receipt email, a thank-you message after a reservation or online order. The closer the ask sits to the meal, the more reviews it produces. Consistency beats intensity: a steady ask at every service outperforms a desperate review drive twice a year, and a steady stream of recent reviews reads as a living business to everyone searching.

Placement

Where should the review link live?

Every extra step between the guest and the review form costs you reviews. Your Google Business Profile gives you a short review link and a QR code. Put them where a guest's eyes and phone already are.

/ 01

QR codes and table tents

A small table tent or a QR sticker at the register and the host stand turns waiting time into review time. Keep the wording neutral: "Tell us about your visit" beats any phrasing that hints at the rating you are hoping for.

/ 02

Receipts and receipt email

Print the short review link or QR code on the receipt, and add it to the receipt email and order confirmations for takeout and delivery. These reach guests after they leave, when the in-person ask is no longer possible.

/ 03

Everywhere the business already talks

Your website's contact page, your social profiles, your confirmation messages. One canonical review link everywhere, so the team never improvises a different one. If the link is hard to find for your staff, it is invisible to your guests.

The other half

Why should you respond to every review?

Because the response is not for the reviewer. It is for the next hundred people deciding where to eat, who read your replies as a preview of how you run the place.

Respond to all of them: the glowing, the unfair, and the two-star "it was fine." Thank people specifically, name the dish or the detail they mentioned, and keep it short. For a negative review, acknowledge the issue without arguing in public, say what you changed if you changed something, and offer a direct way to reach you. A calm, specific reply under a bad review routinely does more for trust than the rating cost you.

Then do the unglamorous part: fix what reviews complain about. Reviews are free operations research. If three guests in a month mention slow bar service on Fridays, that is not a reputation problem, it is a staffing note. If people keep saying they could not find parking, put the answer on your profile and in your replies. Restaurants that treat reviews as a fix list watch their average climb for a real reason: the experience actually improved, and later guests say so.

The lines

What is never allowed when asking for reviews?

Two shortcuts get restaurants in trouble, and both are against Google policy. Say this plainly to your whole team, because one well-meaning server can cross the line.

Never

Buying or paying for reviews

Never buy reviews, pay a service for positive reviews, or offer a discount, dessert or anything else in exchange for one. Incentivized and fake reviews violate Google's policies, they get removed, and they put the listing itself at risk.

Never

Review gating

Never pre-screen guests: no "were you happy?" survey that routes happy guests to Google and unhappy guests to a private form. Selectively soliciting positive reviews is review gating, and Google prohibits it. Ask everyone the same question, send everyone to the same link.

Never

Scripting the guest

Never ask for five stars, hand a guest wording to copy, or have staff and family post reviews as if they were customers. Ask for an honest review, accept what comes, and let the average be whatever your food and service earn.

Where does Magpie fit in this? Briefly and factually: a business can put its Google review link on its Magpie profile, and creators see that link in the visit flow, the same link any guest gets. External reviews are never required on the platform and earn nothing there, and a creator's post about a comped visit follows the same honesty rules as everything else on Magpie. The review system that works is the one you run with every guest, every service, in markets like Nashville and Hampton Roads alike. Pair it with a steady flow of local creator visits from the business plans, and the new guests those posts bring in become the next round of honest reviews.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Can a restaurant buy Google reviews?

No. Buying reviews, paying for positive reviews, or offering a discount or free item in exchange for a review all violate Google's review policies, and fake reviews can be removed and put the listing at risk. The only durable route is asking real guests for honest reviews and making it easy.

What is review gating, and why is it banned?

Review gating means pre-screening guests, for example asking if they were happy first and only sending the happy ones to Google while routing unhappy guests to a private form. Google prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews. Ask every guest the same neutral question and send everyone to the same review link.

When is the best moment to ask for a Google review?

Right after the best moment of the visit: the plates cleared and the guest saying the meal was great, or just after payment, while the experience is fresh and the phone is already in hand. The receipt, the receipt email and a QR code on the table tent carry the ask for guests you miss in person.

Should a restaurant respond to negative Google reviews?

Yes, every one of them, and the positive ones too. Thank the guest, acknowledge the specific issue without arguing, and say what changed or invite them to reach out directly. Future guests read your replies more than the complaint itself, and a pattern of calm, specific responses builds trust.

Do Magpie creators have to leave Google reviews?

No. External reviews are never required on Magpie and earn nothing on the platform. A business can put its Google review link on its Magpie profile, and creators see it in the visit flow like any guest would, but what a creator does off the platform is their own choice.
