How to run a comped creator visit at your business

A comped visit is simple, but simple is not the same as casual. The businesses that get great posts run the same few steps every time: a real offer, a written deliverable, a briefed team, a clean code at the register and a result they count. Here is the full playbook, in order.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 15, 2026 · Magpie Learn hub

The Short Answer

Run a comped creator visit by offering your best experience at real value, putting one post and one story in writing, briefing your staff and using a single use code at the register. You get honest content within forty eight hours and counted results afterward.

Key Takeaways
  • Offer your best experience at real value, because a thin comp produces a thin post.
  • Put 1 post and 1 story, the 48 hour goal and disclosure in writing before the visit.
  • Brief staff, redeem a single use code at the register and count the result every time.
Step one · Offer

What should you offer, and how do you set its value?

Offer your best, not your leftovers. A comped creator is paid in the experience itself, so the experience is both your cost and your advertisement. If you comp the dish you are proudest of, the service that shows your skill, or the experience guests talk about on the drive home, the creator has something real to post about. If you comp the smallest, cheapest version of what you do, the post will feel exactly that small, and you will have saved a little product cost at the price of the whole visit.

Real value does not mean unlimited value. Set the comp and its ceiling clearly on every offer: what is included, for how many people where that applies, and what is not included. A clear ceiling protects your margin and, just as importantly, protects the creator from an awkward moment at the register. On Magpie the business sets the comp, Magpie never touches comp money, and the cost to you is your product cost, not the retail price. For the definition behind this playbook, read what a comped creator visit is.

Choose creators for the offer with local reach in mind. The profile shows the share of a creator audience that lives in your market before you choose, whether your business is in Toronto or any other founding market. A great offer to a creator whose audience cannot visit you is a generous gift, not a marketing visit. Match the best offer to the most local audience and the visit has a chance to pay for itself many times over.

Step two · Writing

What goes in the written deliverable?

Put the deliverable in writing before the visit, every time, even with a creator you know well. The standard visit deliverable is 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post. Add the timing: the goal is posting within 48 hours of the visit. Add the life of the post: kept live for 30 or more days. Add the disclosure: clear FTC disclosure, for example #gifted or a Paid Partnership label. Add the honesty rule in plain words: the post is the creator honest opinion, and a positive review is never promised or required.

Writing protects both sides. The creator knows exactly what finished looks like, so there is no guessing and no scope creep after a good meal. You know exactly what you can expect and count, so a delivered visit is easy to recognize. Most visit problems are not bad faith, they are mismatched expectations that a five line written agreement would have prevented. The briefing craft behind the message itself is covered in how to brief a creator.

Keep the two lanes separate in the writing as well. A comped visit is paid in the experience and carries native share only. A paid booking is a different agreement, at the creator rate, with a license included. Never blend them on one visit, because blended deals are where confusion, resentment and missing posts come from.

Step three · Staff

How do you brief your staff for a creator visit?

Brief the team in two minutes, before the visit, not during it. Who is coming, when, what they were offered, and how the code will be handled at the end. The host or front desk should greet the creator by name and treat the visit like a valued regular first visit: warm, normal and unhurried. Nothing kills a post faster than a creator feeling like an inconvenience, a suspected freeloader or a production problem the staff resents.

Give the visit one owner on your side. That person checks the creator in, makes sure the best version of the experience is delivered, and closes the visit with the code. If a dish is wrong or a service runs late, that owner fixes it the way you would for any guest whose opinion you care about, because the honest post will include how the business handled the moment. Recovery, done well, often becomes the most believable line in the content.

Do not direct the content. Do not ask to approve the post, supply a script or request specific praise. You agreed on a deliverable, not a verdict. Creators who feel trusted post better, post on time and say yes to the next visit, and their audiences can tell when a recommendation is genuinely theirs. Your control is in the offer, the experience and the choice of creator. Use it there, and let the post be theirs.

Step four · Register

How does the in app code work at the register?

The code is what turns a friendly comp into a counted business activity. On Magpie, comps redeem on a single use in app code. At the end of the visit, the creator code is applied, the comp is recorded against that visit, and the visit is marked as delivered on the redemption side. Single use matters: it prevents confusion, double counting and the slow drift where a business can no longer tell which posts came from which visits.

Counting goes further when you connect the register. A business can connect Square so creator codes read back as real register totals. That connection lets you review a visit, and a month of visits, with the same honesty you apply to any other spend: what the comp cost at product cost, what content arrived, and what sales activity followed. Without counting, creator marketing stays a feeling. With counting, it becomes a channel you can improve.

Keep the register moment graceful. The code should feel like a normal, modern checkout step, not like an audit at the table. A briefed cashier, a quick scan or entry, a thank you and a genuine invitation to come back as a paying guest: that is the whole scene. The creator leaves feeling like a customer, which is exactly what their post will describe.

What you get

What do you get after a well run visit?

You get the post and the story, published to a local audience, honest and disclosed, with the goal of going live within 48 hours. You get a place in the creator content library of real photos and video of your business, which you can reshare as native content. You get counted results through the code, so the visit joins your real business records instead of living in a screenshot folder. And you get a relationship: a local creator who has been treated well is a creator who returns, rebooks and recommends you to other creators.

The Visit Guarantee covers the downside. If no post appears inside a week, the visit does not count against your plan and is replaced in credits, not cash. That guarantee is not a reason to skip the steps in this playbook. It is the backstop that lets you run visits generously, with your best offer on the table, knowing a single failed visit will not quietly consume your budget.

Run the steps in order and repeat them. Offer your best at real value, write the deliverable, brief the staff, redeem the code and count the result. After five visits run this way, you will know your cost per post, your most reliable creators and the offers your market responds to. That knowledge, more than any single post, is what a comped visit program is really building.

Straight Answers

What do owners ask about running a comped visit?

What should I offer on a comped creator visit?

Offer your best experience at real value: the meal, service or experience you would give a guest you most want to impress, with a ceiling you set in advance. The creator is paid in that experience, at your product cost, so a generous and genuine offer is both affordable for you and the reason the resulting post feels enthusiastic and honest.

What deliverable should be agreed for a comped visit?

The standard deliverable is 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, honest, with the goal of posting within 48 hours of the visit, kept live for 30 or more days, with clear FTC disclosure such as #gifted or a Paid Partnership label. Put that deliverable in writing before the visit so both sides judge the same finished work.

What happens if a creator does not post after a comped visit?

Under the Magpie Visit Guarantee, a visit with no post inside a week does not count against a business plan and is replaced in credits, not cash. In practice, a clear written deliverable, a briefed welcome and a code redeemed at the register make no shows rare, because the creator knows exactly what was agreed.
