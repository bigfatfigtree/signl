What does local actually mean in Richmond?

Richmond runs on neighborhoods. A creator who is trusted in Church Hill does not automatically carry weight in Scotts Addition, and a Fan audience shops differently from a Manchester or Shockoe Bottom crowd.

Define local by the drive your customers actually make. If you are in Carytown, your best creators post for Carytown, the Fan and nearby Midlothian families who already come in on weekends. If you are in Scotts Addition, you want the brewery, food hall and apartment crowd that lives within a few miles and decides plans on Thursday afternoon. If you are south of the river in Manchester, you need creators whose followers cross the river for dinner, not creators whose audience stops at Broad Street.

That neighborhood test is why national marketplaces disappoint Richmond businesses. They sort by follower count and category, so you get polished product creators who happen to list Richmond as a location. A Richmond business needs something narrower: a person whose recent posts are Richmond places, whose comments come from Richmond people, and whose followers can act on a post this week. On SIGNL, that maps to tiers you can plan around: Neighbors at 500-2k followers, Locals at 2k-15k and Headliners at 15k+. Anyone can apply at any count.

Where to look

Where do Richmond creators actually post?

Richmond has a dense, proud food and small business scene, and its creators leave a clear trail if you search by place instead of by keyword.

/ 01

Neighborhood location tags

Start with Instagram and TikTok location tags for Scotts Addition, Carytown, Church Hill, Shockoe Bottom, Manchester and the Fan. Save every account that posts from 3 or more different local businesses in a month. One visit is tourism. A pattern is a local creator.

/ 02

VCU and the RVA food scene

VCU campus creators reach students, staff and young renters around the Fan and downtown who try new places fast and post in groups. Pair them with established RVA food scene posters who cover openings and seasonal menus. Students drive trial, food regulars drive repeat visits.

/ 03

Reviews and your own tags

Yelp Elite reviewers in Richmond write the long, specific reviews locals trust. Many also post short video on TikTok. Then mine your own account: who tagged you, who was tagged at the table next door, who comments on every new menu? Your regulars are already creating, just without a brief.

Check audience location in 2 minutes per account: read the last 20 comments. Are people asking about parking, hours and cross streets? That is a local audience. Are the comments mostly fire emojis from other creators in other cities? That is a creator network, not a customer base, and it will not fill your dining room in Richmond.

The offer

What should you offer a Richmond creator?

Offer a comp at your own cost and a visit that feels like a real night out, not a staged ad shoot.

The standard Richmond starting point is the same honest deal SIGNL uses everywhere: you comp a meal, service or experience at your cost, the creator visits like a real customer, and they post honestly within 48 hours with FTC disclosure. The Standard Visit is 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, the post stays live 30+ days, and the visit is tracked. No business can require a positive post, and in Richmond that honesty is the point: locals can spot a forced rave instantly.

For paid work, creators set their own rate. Typical guidance is Neighbors $25-75 per post, Locals $75-250 per post and Headliners $250-750 per post. The platform fee is 6% total, split evenly, so a creator keeps 97% of their rate on a paid booking. Agree the deliverable, the window and the disclosure before the visit, and let the creator use their own voice and format.
