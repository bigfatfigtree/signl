What businesses see before they book you: a tour of your own profile

A business does not study your profile. It reads a card, in about five seconds, usually on a phone, and decides whether to keep reading or move to the next creator. This is that card, field by field, in the order a business sees it: your photo, your local reach, your categories, your rate, and your delivery record, plus what each field is quietly saying about you and how to fix the weak ones today.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 19, 2026 · Magpie Learn hub

For creators Your profile is your rate card
The Short Answer

Before a business books you, it reads five things on your profile, in order: your photo, your local reach and local follower count, your categories, your listed rate, and your delivery record. Each one answers a different question, and a blank or vague field answers it badly. Complete beats impressive: a clear profile with modest numbers books ahead of a flashy profile with gaps.

Key Takeaways
  • The read takes about five seconds, on a phone, next to other creators.
  • Local reach is the headline number. Total follower count is a supporting line.
  • A missing rate or a missing category removes you from decisions you never hear about.
  • Your delivery record is the only field you cannot edit. You earn it, one on time post at a time.
The five second read

Picture the moment your profile gets judged

An owner is between other jobs. She has a quiet Tuesday to fill, a new dish to show, or a chair that keeps sitting empty, and she is scrolling a roster of local creators on her phone. She is not looking for a celebrity. She is looking for someone nearby, believable, and easy to book, whose followers could actually walk in this week.

That means your profile is not read like a resume. It is read like a shop window: a fast glance, a couple of details that either build confidence or break it, and a decision. The five fields below are the whole window. It helps to know that this is also what what brands look for in local creators comes down to in practice: local audience first, then fit, then reliability.

The tour

Your profile, field by field, in the order she sees it

/ 01

Your photo: are you a real person I can picture walking in?

Your photo is read first because it does the trust work. A clear photo of you, face visible, in the kind of place you post about, tells a business three things at once: you are real, you are local, and your posts will look like a person, not a logo. A blurry group shot, a cartoon avatar, or a photo so old it no longer looks like you all cost you the same way: the owner cannot picture the visit, so she moves on. You do not need a professional shoot. You need one sharp, current photo where a stranger can see your face.

/ 02

Your local reach: can your audience actually get here?

This is the headline number, and it is your local reach: the share of your audience that lives in the market, shown with the local follower count behind it, so a business can see both the percentage and the people. It answers the only reach question that matters to a local business: of everyone who follows you, how many could walk in. That is why Magpie ranks and surfaces creators by local reach, not follower count. A creator with 3,000 followers who are mostly nearby reads stronger here than a creator with 30,000 followers spread across the country, and the card says so before anyone does math.

/ 03

Your categories: do you post about places like mine?

Categories tell the owner whether your feed fits her business. If she runs a bakery and your categories say food, cafes, and bakeries, the visit is easy to imagine. If your categories are empty, or so narrow they miss the businesses around you, you disappear from reads you would have won. Most creators can honestly post about more than one kind of business, because followers follow the person, not just the subject. Tick every category you can post about honestly, and leave off only the ones you truly would not do.

/ 04

Your rate: can I say yes without starting a negotiation?

A listed rate turns a maybe into a decision. The owner can compare your rate with your local reach, see whether a paid booking fits her budget, and book without opening a long message thread. A missing rate does the opposite: it adds a step, a guess, and a delay, and busy owners usually solve that problem by booking the creator whose price is already on the card. This is the sense in which your creator profile is your rate card. If you have not set yours yet, the guide to what a small creator should charge walks through the numbers. On Magpie a paid booking runs at your own listed rate, with a $50 floor, and you keep 97 percent.

/ 05

Your delivery record: will the post actually happen, on time?

The last field is the one you cannot write yourself. Your delivery record shows whether your past visits turned into posts, and whether those posts arrived on time. To an owner, it is the difference between booking a person and booking a hope. Posting on time earns Momentum points, and Momentum raises your visibility, which is how posting on time gets you booked more. A new creator has a short record, and that is fine. Every creator with a strong record started with a first visit delivered properly.

What is not deciding it

Three things businesses weigh less than you think

  • ✓Your total follower count, on its own: it is context, not the headline. Without a local share behind it, a big number tells a local business almost nothing about who can visit.
  • ✓A wall of past brand logos: useful proof off platform, which is why a one page media kit built from your own posts helps in a cold pitch. On a profile, the live numbers and the delivery record carry more weight than old logos.
  • ✓Perfect production: owners book believable local people, not studios. A clear phone photo of a real visit beats a polished post that could have come from anywhere.

None of this means presentation does not matter. It means the presentation that matters is completeness and clarity: the right five fields, filled in honestly, with numbers you can back up.

The fix list

Read your own profile like a business, in five minutes

Open your profile on your phone, in bright light, the way an owner would. Then work down this list and fix anything that fails:

  • ✓Photo: could a stranger pick you out of a lineup from this photo, and does it still look like you?
  • ✓Local reach: is your audience location information complete, so your local percentage and local count show at their real strength?
  • ✓Categories: does the list cover every kind of business you can honestly post about, not just the one you post about most?
  • ✓Rate: is a rate listed, and is it a rate you would happily accept this week, not a number you set once and outgrew?
  • ✓Record: if you have visits behind you, did each post land on time? If you are new, is everything above ready for the first one?

Four of the five fixes take minutes and cost nothing. The fifth, your record, is built one booking at a time, and it starts with the first visit you deliver exactly as agreed.

Straight Answers

What else do creators ask about their profile?

What do businesses see first on a creator profile?

Your photo, your name and handle, and your local reach. Those three sit at the top of the card a business reads, and they answer the first question in about five seconds: is this a real local person whose audience lives near my business. Everything else on the profile, your categories, your rate, and your delivery record, is read after that first impression lands.

What do brands look for on a creator profile?

Five things, in this order: a clear photo that looks like the person who will walk in, local reach with the local follower count behind it, categories that match the business, a listed rate with no guessing involved, and a delivery record that shows posts arriving on time. A profile that answers all five cleanly gets booked ahead of a bigger profile that leaves any of them blank or vague.

Does total follower count matter more than local reach?

No. On Magpie creators are ranked and discovered by local reach, the share of their audience that lives in the market, not by total follower count. A smaller creator whose audience mostly lives nearby can be the stronger booking for a local business than a much larger creator whose followers are spread across the country, because only the local followers can walk in.

What should I fix first if I am not getting booked?

Fix blanks before you fix polish. Add a clear, current photo if yours is missing or hard to read, make sure your local audience information is complete, tick every category you can honestly post about, and list a rate if the field is empty. A missing rate or a missing category removes you from decisions you never hear about, while a complete profile stays in every read.

How does my delivery record affect future bookings?

Businesses can see whether you post on time, and that record follows you from booking to booking. On Magpie, posting within the standard earns Momentum points, and Momentum raises your visibility to local businesses. One visit delivered on time is a good start. A run of them becomes the reason a business picks you without needing to be convinced.
