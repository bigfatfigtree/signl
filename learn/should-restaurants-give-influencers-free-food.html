Should I give influencers free food for posting?

By Jeff, Founder, SIGNL · October 30, 2026

Sometimes, yes. A comp makes sense when the creator's audience actually lives near your restaurant, the trade is agreed in writing before the visit (what is comped, what gets posted, by when), and the visit is tracked. A comp given on a DM promise with no terms is how owners get burned, and the free-meal spam every restaurant knows is exactly that mistake repeated at scale.

The short answer

Comp the audience, not the account. If the creator's followers live within driving distance of your door, a meal at your cost for an honest post is the cheapest real marketing a restaurant can buy. If they do not live near you, no comp size makes the post worth it.

When it works

When does a comp make sense?

A comp is a marketing spend, and it earns its keep in three situations: slow nights you want to fill, new things you want sampled, and neighborhoods you want to own.

/ 01

Slow nights

A Tuesday table that would sit empty costs you almost nothing to comp: the food cost on a meal, not the menu price. A local creator posting about a Tuesday night reaches the people who can actually come on a Tuesday.

/ 02

New menu, new room, new season

One week of local creators trying a new dish beats one glossy photo shoot, because five real posts from people the neighborhood follows reads as five recommendations, not one ad.

/ 03

Your own neighborhood

The audience that matters is the one that lives near you. A creator with 800 followers in your neighborhood is worth more than a creator with 80,000 followers spread across the country, and the math is on the local reach page.

What is fair

What does a fair comp look like?

A fair comp has four parts: the meal, the ceiling, the post, and the deadline. Leave any one out and the deal starts to drift.

The meal. Most restaurant offers cover 1 or 2 people, such as a meal for two, because creators bring a guest and the guest is part of the experience. The ceiling. State it in the offer: a dollar cap or a set menu, so nobody discovers at the register that the comp was an open tab. The post. On SIGNL the package is fixed, called the Standard Visit: 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, honest, in the creator's own voice, kept live for 30 or more days. The deadline. 48 hours is the standard, one week the outside limit.

What you can never require: a positive post, a script, pre-approval of the opinion, or a good review anywhere else. That line is the whole product, because the day a creator's feed becomes scripted praise, the audience stops believing it. Disclosure is the other non-negotiable: a free meal is a material connection under FTC rules, and the post must say so, for example with #gifted. The full disclosure rules are in FTC disclosure for comped posts.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Do I have to pay cash on top of a free meal?

No. On SIGNL an offer is one lane or the other: a comped visit, where the meal is the pay, or a paid booking at the creator's own listed rate. The two lanes never combine on a single visit, so a comped visit never turns into a bill, and a paid booking never expects a free meal on top.

How many followers should a creator have to earn a comp?

Follower count is the wrong test. The right test is local reach: the share of the creator's audience that lives in your market. On SIGNL, creators with 500 followers and a mostly local audience regularly beat much larger accounts whose followers live elsewhere, because only the local audience can walk in this week.

What if the creator never posts after the free meal?

Off a platform, your remedy is a polite follow-up and a lesson: agree the deliverable in writing next time. On SIGNL, the visit is redeemed in the app, the post is tracked to a 48-hour standard with one week as the outside limit, and a visit with no post does not count against your plan and is replaced under the Visit Guarantee.

Is it legal to trade food for posts?

Yes, with disclosure. A free meal is a material connection under FTC rules, so the creator must clearly disclose that the visit was comped, for example with #gifted. The business shares responsibility for making sure the disclosure happens, which is why disclosure is part of tracked delivery on SIGNL rather than left to chance.

What is a fair comp for one post?

A meal for one or two people, at your cost, with a stated ceiling, in exchange for the Standard Visit: 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, posted to a 48-hour standard and kept live for 30 or more days. A comp with no ceiling, no deadline and no defined post is not a deal, it is an open tab.
