Black Friday marketing for local businesses

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · October 22, 2026

A local business wins Black Friday by being the place its own neighborhood is talking about that week, not by out-discounting a chain. The practical play: put local creators in your seats during the week of November 23-27, let their honest posts land while neighbors are making plans, and give each creator a code so the register can tally what the posts brought in. Here is the plan, week by week.

The problem

Why can't a local business win a discount war?

Chains buy attention nationally and discount from margins a local business does not have. Matching their prices trains your customers to wait for sales and gives away the one week when people are ready to spend. The local advantage is different, and Black Friday is when it is worth the most.

Them

What chains have

National ad budgets, loss-leader pricing, and a logo everyone already knows. Their Black Friday game is reach and depth of discount. A local business playing that game is playing away from home, at a discount.

You

What you have

Proximity, regulars, and a room people can actually picture themselves in. Your next hundred customers live within a few miles and take recommendations from people they know. That trust cannot be bought by the chain across town.

The play

Be talked about locally

Fill the week with honest posts from local creators whose followers are your neighbors. A post about your table, your shelf or your chair, from someone local, lands while the weekend is being planned. That is the whole strategy.

The offer

What is the Black Friday Burst?

The Black Friday Burst is the seasonal form of Magpie's Grand Opening Burst: a one-time $1,500 add-on that concentrates a wave of creator visits into one big moment. Built for a launch, aimed at the biggest retail week of the year.

For Black Friday, the wave runs across November 23-27: creators visit on the days you choose inside that window, and the goal is for every post to land within 48 hours of its visit, while neighbors are still deciding where to eat, shop and book. Burst bookings close Monday, November 16, so creators, windows and offers can be set before the week starts. The Burst sits on top of a plan, and plan visits are metered the usual way: Self-Service at $199 a month includes up to 5 creator visits a month, Growth at $349 includes up to 10, and Managed at $699 includes up to 20, each with a 3-month minimum and then month to month. The full grid is on the business pricing page, and restaurants can see the category view on Magpie for restaurants.

The comp itself stays yours: your product, at your cost, with a ceiling you set on every offer. And the Visit Guarantee still applies, so a creator who does not show up or does not post does not burn one of your visits. The visit is replaced.

Why the window matters

A post about your business on November 20 is planning. A post on December 2 is a recap. The November 23-27 window is the week neighbors are choosing Friendsgiving tables, gift stops and Small Business Saturday routes. Visits inside the window put your name in those conversations while the decisions are still open.

The calendar

How do you prepare, week by week?

Five short moves, one per week. None of them takes more than an hour, and all of them are cheaper than a single weekend of discounted margin.

Now to November 2: decide the offer. Pick the comp, the visit windows you want covered, and the one thing you want talked about: a new menu, a gift card worth showing off, a set Black Friday menu, a service with holiday slots. One clear offer beats a vague invitation.

November 3-16: book the wave. Get on the founding business list, or book the Burst by the November 16 close. Creators claim their windows, and you can see the week take shape instead of hoping for walk-ins.

November 17-22: get the room ready. Brief the staff: which creators are coming, what their codes look like, how redemption works at the counter. Put gift cards where the posts will point people. A creator visit a team is expecting runs smoothly and photographs that way too.

November 23-27: visits land. Creators visit, posts aim to go live within 48 hours of each visit, and codes start tallying at the register. Share the posts natively as they land. That share is included with every comped visit; anything beyond it, like reposting to your own feed or running a post as an ad, comes with paid visits, where the creator is booked at their rate and ads run for 90 days at most, under the standard license in the booking. The creator always owns their content.

November 28 and after: read the tally. Small Business Saturday catches the spillover, then the register tally tells you which creators actually moved spend. The method for reading that number honestly is in how to track influencer marketing ROI, and the same creators, booked again in December, are how a holiday week becomes a regulars list.

The follow-through

What makes the week convert?

Attention is only half of it. The week converts when the posts have somewhere to land: a code at the register, a gift card that sells December in November, and a team that knows the plan.

Every creator carries a unique code their followers can use, and your register tallies the spend those codes bring in. That tracked spend is a floor, since it only counts guests who used a code, and it is the number that tells you the week worked. Gift cards deserve a special push: a creator post that sells a gift card is a December visit bought in November, by a customer who may never have walked in otherwise.

The same machinery that runs this week runs all year. It starts with the comped creator visit itself: a defined offer, a tracked visit, an honest disclosed post, with the goal of landing inside 48 hours of the visit, and a replacement if it does not happen. Black Friday just concentrates it into the five days when the whole neighborhood is deciding at once.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

When is Black Friday 2026, and when should a local business start?

Black Friday falls on Friday, November 27, 2026, and Small Business Saturday follows on November 28. Start now: the work that matters, booking creators, setting the offer and briefing staff, happens in the weeks before. On Magpie, Black Friday Burst bookings close Monday, November 16, so the visit wave can be scheduled into the November 23-27 window.

What is the Black Friday Burst?

It is the seasonal form of Magpie's Grand Opening Burst: a one-time $1,500 add-on that concentrates a wave of creator visits into one big moment. For Black Friday, visits run across November 23-27, so honest, disclosed posts land while neighbors are deciding where to eat, shop and book. Your plan's monthly visits and the Visit Guarantee still apply.

How much does Black Friday creator marketing cost beyond the Burst?

Your plan: $199 a month for Self-Service (up to 5 creator visits a month), $349 for Growth (up to 10) or $699 for Managed (up to 20), with a 3-month minimum, then month to month. The comp itself is your own product at your cost, never at retail, and paid creator bookings are optional extras at the creator's listed rate.

Do the creators have to post positive things about my business?

No, never. Creators post honestly, in their own voice, with FTC disclosure, with the goal of posting within 48 hours of the visit. A business sets the offer, the window and the deliverables; it cannot require a positive post or approve the creator's opinion. Honest posts are what neighbors believe, and that honesty is the whole point.

How do I know whether the week worked?

Each creator carries a unique code their followers can use, and your register tallies the spend those codes bring in. That tracked spend is a floor, not a total: it counts only the guests who used a code. Engagement value is modeled separately and labeled as modeled.
