What does local actually mean in Austin?

Austin has two audiences: the festival crowd that flies in a few weekends a year, and the neighbors who fill your dining room the other fifty weeks. Local means the second one, with proof.

Start by drawing your real trade area. An East Austin food truck pulls a different crowd than a South Congress boutique or a Domain restaurant, and a creator who is perfect for one can be wrong for the others, even at the same follower count, because Austin loyalty is neighborhood loyalty. This matters more in Austin than almost anywhere: the festival and event economy inflates follower counts with people who were here for one long weekend.

So judge creators by audience location inside that trade area. Look for recent posts in your neighborhood's places, comments from people naming local streets and dishes, and stories that show ordinary local life between the festivals. On SIGNL this is the headline number, called local reach: the share of a creator's audience that actually lives here. Plan by tier as well as place: Neighbors at 500-2k followers for everyday word of mouth, Locals at 2k-15k for regular local posters, and Headliners at 15k+ for launches.

Where to look

Where do Austin creators actually post?

They cluster around three engines: neighborhood tags, a food-and-music scene that runs every night of the week, and campuses that reset the audience every fall.

/ 01

Location tags by neighborhood

Search Instagram and TikTok location tags for South Congress, East Austin, Rainey Street, Zilker, Hyde Park, South Lamar and The Domain first. Save accounts that post local food, fitness, beauty and family content repeatedly, in ordinary weeks, not just during SXSW or ACL.

/ 02

The food truck and live music bench

Austin's creator depth is a food story and a music story at once: barbecue lines, taco trucks, coffee trailers, and a live music calendar that fills rooms on Tuesday nights. The accounts that matter post those rooms and trucks in normal weeks, and their comments fill with locals trading their own orders. If you run a venue, those Tuesday-night creators are your bench, and SIGNL books musicians as creators too.

/ 03

Campus creators and your own regulars

University of Texas, St. Edward's University and Austin Community College creators reach students and young alumni in exactly the neighborhoods around their campuses. Then look closer to home: your tagged customers, your reviewers, and the neighborhood accounts your regulars already trust. A trusted regular with 900 followers can start the group chat that fills your Saturday.

Use a 10 minute vetting pass: open the profile, check the last 12 posts for Austin places, read comments for local intent, and confirm the creator posts in their own voice. If every post is a brand deal for a shipped product, they are a product creator who lives in Austin, not a local creator Austin follows for local choices.

The offer

What should you offer an Austin creator?

Offer a comp at your own cost, a clear visit window, and freedom to post honestly. That combination gets accepted and gets posted on time.

On SIGNL, businesses comp local creators a meal, service or experience at the business cost. The creator posts honestly within 48 hours with FTC disclosure. The Standard Visit is 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, and delivery is tracked to the post. A post inside a week still counts as delivered; 48 hours is the standard the creator is held to. For an East Austin opening or a new South Lamar menu, that structure gives you multiple local voices in the same week without scripting any of them.

If you book paid posts instead, creators set their own rate, paid bookings start at a $50 floor, and the platform fee is 6% total, split evenly, so the creator keeps 97% of their rate. A paid booking includes one standard license: 90 days of repost and ad use, inside the booking terms. State clearly whether the booking is comp only or paid, and never trade a comp for a promised positive review.
