Magpie vs Mustard

Mustard and Magpie both exist to get local creators through local doors. Mustard sells automated campaigns, priced by how many creators you run each month, in hundreds of cities. Magpie builds one roster per city and lets you choose from it by local reach. Here is how the two approaches compare, from their pricing page and ours.

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 15, 2026 · Magpie Learn hub

The Short Answer

Mustard and Magpie both bring local creators into local businesses, but they run differently. Mustard sells automated campaign plans priced per month by creator count, with compensation you fund on top. Magpie is a city roster you choose from by local reach, with comped visits or paid bookings at each creator's own rate, at published founding pricing.

Key Takeaways
  • Mustard is campaign software: you launch a campaign, creators apply, its AI matches or you handpick, and plans are priced by how many creators run each month.
  • Mustard publishes its prices: $299 a month for 3 creators on the managed campaign plan, $499 for 5 and $999 for 10, plus a $99 a month self-service tier with $25 per booking; creator compensation is extra.
  • Magpie is a standing roster, not a campaign blast: you choose creators by the local reach on each profile, and visits are comped or booked at the creator's own rate.
The other platform

What is Mustard today?

Mustard is a local influencer marketing platform that started with restaurants and now markets to local businesses broadly: its homepage speaks to restaurants plus shops, hotels, beauty, fitness and events businesses. The model is campaign based. You set up a campaign, local creators apply, and you handpick from the applications or let its AI match. Mustard guarantees at least 3 applications per campaign and refunds unfulfilled slots, and it says its network holds more than 175,000 creators across more than 400 cities.

On its managed plans, the Mustard team helps with outreach, scheduling and follow-ups. On its self-service tier, you run invites, scheduling, reminders and approvals yourself. Creator compensation is flexible: food, cash or affiliate commission, and cash compensation sits on top of the plan price. Mustard sells breadth and automation: a campaign can be live quickly, in almost any city, with the software doing the chasing.

The honest part

Where Mustard is the better fit

If you want creators this week, in a city where Magpie has not launched, Mustard is the practical pick. Its network is already broad, its campaigns are automated end to end, and its guarantee of applications per campaign takes the scariest part of a first campaign, silence, off the table. For a group running the same campaign across many cities at once, that breadth is the product.

Choose Magpie if you want the opposite shape: fewer, known creators in your own city, chosen on local reach data you can read before you commit, who come back month after month as regulars. A roster compounds differently than a campaign. The tenth visit from a creator your customers recognize is worth more than the first, and that is the asset Magpie is built to create. How Magpie works

What working with Magpie looks like instead

You browse the roster for your city and pick creators by the local reach on each profile, shown as a percentage and as an absolute local follower count. A comped visit earns 1 post and 1 story. A paid booking runs at the rate on the creator profile; the creator keeps 97 percent, the 3 percent difference is card processing, and the booking includes one 90-day license to reuse the post. Nothing is priced per application or per booking on top of the subscription.

Magpie is forming founding rosters now in Hampton Roads, Nashville, Austin, Miami, Toronto, Los Angeles and New York City, covering restaurants and bars, salons and barbershops, fitness studios, retail, attractions and local services. Anyone in any city can sign up, and bookings attach to each market as it launches.

Straight Answers

Magpie vs Mustard, in short

What is the difference between Magpie and Mustard?

Mustard sells automated campaign plans: you launch a campaign, creators apply, and its plans are priced by how many creators run each month, with creator compensation funded on top. Magpie is a standing roster in your city: you choose creators by the local reach shown on each profile, and visits are comped experiences or paid bookings at the creator's own rate, under a published monthly subscription.

How much does Mustard cost?

As published on mustard.love in November 2026: campaign plans are $299 a month for 3 creators, $499 for 5 and $999 for 10, and a self-service tier is $99 a month plus $25 per approved booking. Creator compensation, whether food, cash or affiliate commission, is on top of the plan price. Magpie founding pricing is $199, $349 or $699 a month, published on this website.

Is Mustard only for restaurants?

It began with restaurants and much of its content still leans on food, but its site today markets to local businesses broadly, including shops, hotels, beauty and fitness businesses. Magpie covers restaurants and bars plus salons and barbershops, fitness studios, retail, attractions and local services in its core markets.
