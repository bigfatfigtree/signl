Are comped meals and free services taxable income?

By Jeff, Founder, SIGNL · October 30, 2026

Yes. In the United States, a comped meal or free service you receive in exchange for posting is taxable income at its fair market value: the IRS treats it as payment in kind, not as a tax-free gift. That is true whether or not anyone sends you a tax form, and self-employment tax can apply on top of income tax. This page is general information for creators, not tax advice for your specific situation.

More creator answers How comped meals work
The short answer

A comp is income at its normal price. A $70 dinner comped for a post is $70 of income, a $120 salon service is $120. Report it whether or not a 1099 shows up, and keep a simple log of date, business and value as you go.

Why it counts

Why is a free meal income at all?

Because you were paid, just not in cash. The tax code taxes barter the same way it taxes money: when a business gives you something of value because you posted, the value of what you received is your income. Calling it "gifted" in the caption does not change what it is on a return.

This catches creators out because the money version of the same deal feels obvious. If a restaurant paid you $70 cash to post, nobody would wonder whether it was income. A $70 dinner handed over instead of the cash is the same transaction with a different payment method, and the IRS has treated payment in kind as income for as long as barter has existed.

The same logic covers the whole comped economy: salon services, fitness classes, hotel stays, products sent for review. If it arrived because you create content, its fair market value (what the business normally charges for it) is the number that counts.

Forms and thresholds

What are the numbers to know?

Three numbers cover most creator situations: the 1099-NEC reporting threshold, the $400 self-employment filing threshold, and the 15.3 percent self-employment tax rate.

/ 01

The 1099 threshold

Businesses report payments for services on Form 1099-NEC. The reporting threshold is $600 for tax year 2025, rising to $2,000 for payments made in 2026. A business may report a comp as payment; many will not think of it. Your obligation to report the income does not depend on their paperwork.

/ 02

The $400 rule

If your net earnings from self-employment reach $400 in a year, you must file a tax return even if you owe no income tax. A creator doing a comped visit or two a month crosses that line faster than they expect, because comp values add up at menu prices.

/ 03

Self-employment tax

Creator income is self-employment income, so the 15.3 percent self-employment tax generally applies on top of any income tax. The practical takeaway: set a slice of your paid bookings aside as you earn, and treat comp values as part of the same pot when you estimate.

None of this is a reason to skip comped work. It is a reason to log it. FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Do I owe tax on a comped meal if nobody sent me a form?

Yes. The income is taxable whether or not a 1099 arrives. A form is a reporting tool, not what creates the tax. If you received a meal, service or product in exchange for posting, its fair market value is income for that year, and it belongs on your return.

How do I value a comped meal or service?

At fair market value: what the business normally charges for it. A comped dinner is the menu price of what you were served, a comped haircut is the salon's listed price for that service. Write the value down when the visit happens, because reconstructing it in April is the hard way.

Do I pay self-employment tax on comped visits?

Generally yes, when creating is your self-employment activity. Comped income counts toward net self-employment earnings the same as cash, and the self-employment tax rate is 15.3 percent. If your net self-employment earnings reach $400 in a year, you must file a return even if you owe no income tax.

Will the business send me a 1099 for comps?

It can. Businesses report payments for services on Form 1099-NEC, and the reporting threshold is $600 for tax year 2025, rising to $2,000 for payments made in 2026. Whether a given business reports a comp depends on its own bookkeeping, which is exactly why you keep your own records and report the income either way.

What records should a creator keep for comped visits?

Four things per visit: the date, the business, what you received, and its normal price. On SIGNL your completed visits are already listed in your account history, which covers the date and the business; add the value of each comp as you go and your year-end total takes minutes, not a weekend.
