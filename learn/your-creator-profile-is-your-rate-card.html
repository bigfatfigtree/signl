Your creator profile is your rate card

Your profile · The pitch that works while you sleep
The short answer

Why does a profile beat a pitch?

A pitch reaches one business, once, if the message gets opened. A profile sits in the roster where every business in your market browses, and it answers the same questions every time, in the same order, without you rewriting them. That is what a rate card has always done for working creators: it puts the offer and the price in front of the buyer before the conversation starts.

On SIGNL, businesses discover creators by local reach, not follower count. Your profile is where that number lives, alongside your categories, your rate, and your Momentum record for on-time posting. If those fields are blank, you are not being judged and rejected. You are simply not showing up as a real option. For the number that leads the profile, read what local reach is and why brands care more than follower count.

What businesses read first

What does a business check in the first ten seconds?

Owners are busy and they scan. They are not studying your profile like a portfolio review. They are checking whether you fit, fast, and moving on if the answer is not obvious.

  • ✓Photo and handles: a clear photo and the handles you actually post from, so a business can see your real work in one tap.
  • ✓Market and categories: where you are based and what you post about, stated plainly. A business books a category fit, not a generalist hope.
  • ✓Local reach: the share of your audience that lives in your market. It is the headline number, and a blank or vague version of it weakens the whole card.
  • ✓Rate: your paid rate, visible, if you want paid bookings. No rate tells a business to guess, skip, or start a negotiation most owners will not start.
  • ✓Momentum: your record for posting on time. It starts empty for everyone and builds with every visit you deliver properly.

That scan is the whole first impression. For what sits behind it, read what brands actually look for in local creators and how Momentum gets you booked more.

Fill it in

How do you turn a blank profile into a rate card?

/ 01

Say what you cover

Pick the categories you actually post about and write a short bio in plain words: what you cover, where you are based, and who follows you. Specific beats clever. A business should be able to picture the post.

/ 02

Put your reach on it

Report your local reach honestly. A smaller number that is real and concentrated beats a big follower count with no local share behind it, because local businesses can only sell to people who can visit.

/ 03

Set your rate

If you want paid work, set the rate on the profile instead of waiting to be asked. The guidance in how much a small creator should charge will help you land on a number you can defend.

Do those three and the profile stops being a sign up form and starts being a working document. Every later visit, post, and paid booking adds proof to the same page, so the card gets stronger without you resending anything.

The rate on the card

Why publish a rate instead of negotiating every time?

Because the negotiation is where small deals go to die. A busy owner who has to message to ask a price usually does not. A visible rate lets the right businesses say yes fast and lets the wrong budget filter itself out quietly. You keep control either way: the rate is yours to set and yours to change as your record grows.

On SIGNL the terms are stated plainly. You set your own rate for paid bookings, paid bookings start at a $50 floor, and you keep 97 percent. Comped visits sit alongside that lane, not instead of it: a meal, service, or experience in exchange for the standard post and story. A complete profile presents both lanes at once, which is exactly why creators who fill theirs in get considered for both. The wider playbook is in how to get brand deals with a small following.

Both lanes

How does a complete profile help with comped visits too?

A comped visit is 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, posted within 48 hours, honest and disclosed. Businesses offer those visits to profiles that make the fit obvious, because the offer still costs them product, staff time, and a slot another creator could take.

So the same fields do double work. Categories show the fit. Local reach shows the audience. Momentum, once you have earned some, shows you will post on time. And a pitch still has its place for a business you want specifically; how to pitch local businesses as a small creator covers that. The profile is what the pitch should point to, not a replacement for having one ready. For the visit itself, read what a comped creator visit actually includes.

Creator FAQ

What else do creators ask about profiles and rates?

What makes a creator profile work like a rate card?

A profile works like a rate card when a business can see, in one place, who you are, what you post about, where your audience lives, how reliably you post, and what you charge for a paid booking. When those answers are on the profile, a business does not need a pitch deck or a long message thread to decide whether you fit.

What should a creator put on their profile so brands can find them?

Put a clear photo, your handles, your market and home city, the categories you actually post about, your local reach, and your paid rate if you want paid bookings. Add a short bio that says what you cover and who follows you. Incomplete fields are the main reason a bookable creator gets skipped.

Should creators publish their rate on their profile?

Yes, if you want paid bookings. On SIGNL you set your own rate, paid bookings start at a $50 floor, and you keep 97 percent. A visible rate saves both sides a negotiation, and a business that cannot pay your rate can still consider you for a comped visit where the fit is right.

How do brands find creators on SIGNL?

Businesses browse and search the roster in their market and discover creators by local reach, not follower count. They check your profile, your categories, your rate, and your Momentum record for on-time posting. A complete profile puts all of that in front of them before you ever speak.

Does a complete profile help with comped visits too?

Yes. A comped visit is 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, posted within 48 hours, honest and disclosed. Businesses offer comped visits to profiles that make the fit obvious: the right category, a local audience, and a clear sense of what you post. A thin profile gives them nothing to match against.
