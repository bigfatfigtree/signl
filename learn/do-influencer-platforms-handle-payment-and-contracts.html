Do local influencer platforms handle payment and contracts, or just introductions?

By Jeff, Founder, Magpie · November 16, 2026

It depends on the platform, and the difference matters. Introduction boards hand you a list of names and leave the money, the terms and the chasing to you. Magpie was built the other way: the booking carries the terms, payment runs through the platform and is held until the post is live, and the delivered post sits in the same record as the visit that produced it.

What the platform carries

Three jobs an introduction leaves with you

Ask any platform what happens after it gives you a creator's name. If the answer is a shrug, it sold you introductions. Here is what Magpie carries instead.

/ One

The terms

Every offer states its lane up front: a comped visit or a paid booking, the deliverable of one post plus one story, and the comp or the rate. The creator claims the offer knowing the full terms, and the business posts them knowing the same. Nothing is left to a DM thread.

/ Two

The payment

On a paid booking the business pays when it books, the platform holds the money, and the creator is paid when the post is live. The creator keeps 97 percent of their listed rate; 3 percent is card processing. A comped visit has no payment to mishandle: the experience is the pay.

/ Three

The record

The booking, the visit and the delivered post are one record, so tracking is not a spreadsheet you maintain. A paid booking's record also carries its 90-day license: the right to repost the post and run it as an ad for 90 days, never perpetuity.

The honest contrast

When an introduction is all you get

Directories and databases have their place: finding names. The trouble starts when the name is where the service ends.

With an introduction, everything after the hello is yours. You negotiate the price in messages, agree the deliverable in words, pay a stranger on trust, and then chase the post. If the creator's audience turns out to live in another state, that is your discovery to make. If the post never appears, your contract is a chat thread. Plenty of businesses run exactly this way and absorb the losses as the cost of marketing.

Magpie's answer is to make the booking do the work a contract and a payment processor would do separately. The terms are set in the booking, in the open, before the visit: the lane, the deliverable, the comp or the rate, and the license on paid bookings. Payment for a paid booking runs through the platform and is held until the post is live, which is the same protection from the other side, as how creators make sure they get paid explains. And because the booking, the visit and the post are one record, the tracking question answers itself. The money side of a visit is priced in what it costs to book a local micro influencer.

Straight Answers

Questions, answered straight

Do local influencer platforms handle payment and contracts, or just introductions?

It varies, and it is the first question to ask any platform. Some are introduction boards: names and contact details, with the terms and money left to you. On Magpie the booking sets the terms, payment runs through the platform and is held until the post is live, and the visit and the post are tracked in one record.

Do I need a separate contract for a creator visit?

On Magpie, no separate paperwork for a standard visit. The booking itself sets the terms both sides accept before the visit: the lane, the deliverable of one post plus one story, the comp or the listed rate, and, on paid bookings, one 90-day license. Both sides see the same terms before anyone travels.

How does tracking work after the visit?

The booking, the visit and the delivered post live in one record on Magpie, so you can see what was agreed, what was paid and what was posted without chasing screenshots. A visit with no post does not count, and is replaced.
