Creator and business FAQ

Common questions.

What is Magpie?

Influencer marketing, but local. Magpie is a local creator marketing platform: local businesses comp local creators a free meal, service or experience, or pay the creator's own rate, and the creator posts honestly about the visit on social media, with the goal of posting within 48 hours of the visit. Every creator on the roster lives in the city they post about, every visit is tracked, and Magpie is free for creators. Always.

Does it cost anything to join Magpie as a creator?

No. Magpie is free for creators. Always. Businesses pay the platform. You never pay to join, to list, or to get booked. Your compensation on standard visits is the comp itself: the meal, service or experience the business provides.

What does Magpie charge on paid bookings?

Joining Magpie and comped visits are always free for creators. On paid bookings the platform fee is 6 percent total, split evenly, so creators keep 97 percent of their rate. Paid bookings are held by Magpie until the post is live.

How do creators get paid on Magpie?

On standard visits, the comp is the pay: a free meal, service or experience in exchange for an honest post. Headliners (15,000+ followers) are also eligible for featured paid campaigns when businesses run them, paid in cash on top of comps. Any creator, at any tier, can also set a paid rate on their profile. When a business books you directly at your rate, you keep 97 percent of it, because Magpie takes only 3 percent of the creator's rate.

When do I actually get paid?

On a paid booking the business pays up front, and you are paid after your post is live and verified, within 48 hours, once your payout details are set up. No invoices, no chasing, and no Net 30, 60 or 90 day terms. If the visit or the post never lands, the business is refunded in full instead.

Can a business make me revise my post again and again?

No. Every visit has one defined deliverable, agreed before you claim: 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post, in your own voice. The brief is written up front, so what is expected is never a surprise, and no business can script your words or demand endless edits.

What are the disclosure rules?

Every post about a comped visit must clearly disclose that the visit was comped, as FTC guidelines require: say it in the caption or on screen, and a tag like #gifted is the common shorthand. Disclosure is not optional and it is not fine print; it is what makes your post trustworthy to your followers and safe for you.

What is the 48-hour rule, exactly?

48 hours is the goal: from the moment your visit happens, the goal is for your Standard Visit to be live within 48 hours of your visit: 1 post or reel plus 1 story that shares the post on your own account, with disclosure. The post stays live for at least 30 days. Life happens, so the outside limit is one week: a post inside the week still counts as delivered, just late, while posting inside 48 hours is what earns Momentum. The timing matters because a business comping a visit on Friday needs the post while the weekend is still being planned, not next month. The 48-hour goal applies to comped visits; paid bookings carry the posting terms agreed for that booking.

What happens if I miss the 48 hours, or do not post?

A post inside the week still counts as delivered, so post it as soon as you can: late beats silent every time. What costs you is the week passing with no post and no word from you: then the business gets a replacement visit that does not count against its plan, and your roster standing takes the hit. A no-show with no word from you is a strike, and two strikes remove you from the platform. If something real goes wrong, message us before the week closes; standing is about reliability, and reliability includes communicating.

What are the standards for late posts and no-shows?

Here is the whole ladder. Post within 48 hours of your visit: on time, full Momentum. Post within 7 days: late. It still counts as delivered, but it earns no on-time Momentum, and it goes on your on-time record, which is part of how you are ranked and how much businesses see you, so late posts mean you show up less. Three late posts in a row pauses your new offers for 14 days. No post at all after 7 days is a no-show: the business gets a replacement visit that does not count against its plan, we can give the visit to another creator, and your account is paused, with no new offers, until it is resolved. If you told the business or us about a problem, a no-show is not a strike. If you went silent, it is a strike. Two strikes and you are removed from the platform. And one line is absolute: if you show up, use the comp, and never post at all, you do not stay active. One month after the visit, a creator who consumed the comp and posted nothing is removed from the active roster and is no longer eligible for further opportunities, while a post that lands any time within that month still resolves the visit as delivered, extremely late. Note what this ladder covers: the 48-hour goal and these standards apply to comped visits. Paid bookings carry the posting terms agreed for that booking, set by the business, shown to you and accepted by you before the visit, and those agreed terms govern the paid visit. On a paid booking you are not paid until your post is live, so a paid visit with no post simply does not pay.

What if a business cancels on me, or is not there when I arrive?

You never owe a post for a visit that did not happen. Tell us in the app and the visit will not count against your standing. On paid bookings, payment is held by Magpie until the post is live, so a visit that never lands is refunded in full, and you are never left chasing a business for money.

Do I have to post something positive?

Never. Honest posts only, in your own voice. A business cannot require a positive post or review, cannot ask you to soften an honest one, and cannot retaliate when a post is fair and true. A business that pressures a creator is removed from the platform.

Can a business ask me to delete or change my post?

A business can flag a post it believes is factually false, and Magpie reviews those with both sides. It cannot ask you to change an honest opinion, and honest opinions are never removed for being unflattering.

How do tiers work, and can I move up?

Your tier is set by follower count: Neighbors (500 to 2,000), Locals (2,000 to 15,000) and Headliners (15,000+). Magpie sets your band automatically from your total audience across platforms, so nobody picks their own band, and your tier moves up as your following grows. Every tier gets comps and paid bookings at your own rate; Headliners are first in line for featured paid campaigns and first pick of grand openings and launches.

How many followers do I need to join Magpie?

Magpie starts at 500 total followers across your platforms. Your counts stack across Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube, X and LinkedIn, and your tier is set by that total and rises as you grow. A small, genuinely local audience beats a big, scattered one, because businesses on Magpie shop by audience location, not raw count.

What is my local audience percentage, and who sees it?

It is the share of your followers who live in your Magpie market, and it is the number businesses weigh most when choosing creators. Profiles label where the figure comes from: verified from your own Instagram audience data when you connect it, or self-reported until then. A 1,400-person audience that is 70 percent local beats a 200,000-person audience scattered across a country, and Magpie is built to prove it.

How do referrals and Momentum points work?

Momentum points are your priority score, and you earn them two ways. By delivering: an on-time post is +20, and completed visits, streaks and ratings add more. And by building the roster: your profile carries a personal invite link, and every creator who joins through it is credited to you, +3 when they join and +10 when they are approved. Points are part of the order businesses see when they choose creators, so the people who deliver and build the roster get seen first.

My city is not in the founding markets. Can I still join?

Yes. Apply in the app with your real home city. If your city is not one of the 45 founding markets, your sign-up starts its list: cities are picked by signup counts, and when your city opens, its early applicants become its founding roster, in order.

Is Magpie a UGC creator program?

Partly, with a difference. In a typical UGC program you produce content and hand it to a brand to post on its own channels. On Magpie you post to your own audience as a local creator, which is why local businesses value it: your followers trust you. Delivered posts are also saved to the business's content library. On a comp visit the business can share your post natively and nothing more; repost rights and ad use come only with paid visits, and ads run for 90 days at most, under the standard license in the booking. You always own your content.

How long does review take?

Every founding application gets a decision or a clear status within 2 business days. Applications are reviewed by a person, in sign-up order. If a decision needs more time, you still get a clear status inside that window.

What do I get today, before my market opens?

Three things, starting the day you finish your profile. Your creator page is live and on Google that day. Your paid rate and licensing fee are public on your page, so businesses see your price before they book you. And your founding order is recorded: founding creators see new offers first when their market opens.

Are my rates public on Magpie?

Yes. The paid rate and licensing fee you set show on your public creator page, so businesses see your price before they book you. When a business books you at your rate, you keep 97 percent of it.

Can I bring a guest on a comped visit?

Check the offer before you go. The offer states what is covered, how many people it covers where that applies, and its ceiling. Many offers cover a meal for two, so a guest is included in those. Anything you order or book beyond what the offer covers and its ceiling is on you, so ask before the visit if you are not sure.

How long do I have to keep my post up?

Keep your post live for at least 30 days. That is part of the standard visit terms you agree to before the visit. Taking a post down early can affect your standing, and on a paid booking it can affect payment under the agreed terms for that booking. If you need to edit a factual error, fix the fact and keep the post up.

Is a comped meal or service taxable? Do I get tax paperwork for paid bookings?

This is general information, not tax advice. In the United States, a comped meal or service you receive for posting is generally treated as income at its normal price, and paid booking fees are income too. That is true whether or not a form arrives. Keep a simple record of the date, business, what you received, and its value. Year end forms are issued for paid earnings where the law requires them. Read Are Comped Meals Taxable on Magpie Learn for the full guide, and check your own tax professional for your situation.

Getting started

What types of businesses can use Magpie?

Almost any local business people visit in person can use Magpie, across dining, cafes, nightlife, beauty, health, fitness, retail, experiences, hospitality, events, and visual services. If your customers are local and what you do shows well on camera, it fits. Health and aesthetic posts are personal experiences only, with disclosure and no treatment claims.

Is Magpie live in my city, and what do I do if it is not yet?

A market opens when its roster is ready. The launch line is 50 approved creators in that market. Founding sign ups are open in every city, so you can apply today, lock founding pricing, and get first pick of the roster when your market opens. If your market does not open, you get a full refund.

How does Magpie work? How do I actually get creators to post about my business?

You browse the roster in your market, choose creators, and send a comp offer or a paid booking. The creator visits like a real customer, then posts 1 post and 1 story about the visit, with the goal of posting within 48 hours. On the Managed plan, a coordinator picks creators and schedules visits for you. Every visit is tracked in your dashboard.

Do I need a platform, or can I just DM creators myself?

You can DM creators yourself. If you already know a couple of creators and they post reliably, direct messages work fine. Magpie helps when you need to find new local creators, see their local audience and delivery record before you choose, set clear terms in writing, hold payment until the post is live, and track the result in one place instead of chasing messages.

Does Magpie handle the payment, contracts and tracking for me, or is that on me?

Magpie handles it for you. The offer states the deliverable, timing, and disclosure in writing before the visit. For paid bookings, Magpie holds the money until the post is live. For comped visits, the code at check in ties the visit to the post. Your dashboard tracks each booking as checked in, posts live, and redeemed, so you are not managing separate contracts and spreadsheets.

Can I use Magpie for a grand opening, launch or special event?

Yes. You can book several nearby creators in one go for an opening, launch, or special event with Book the Block. Each creator visits and posts on the same standard terms, with the goal of posting within 48 hours. It is a good fit when you want a group of local posts in a short window instead of visits spread across the month.

Cost and plans

How much does influencer marketing cost for a small business, and what is my total cost on Magpie?

Hired directly, small creators average about $200 a post, and managed quotes commonly run $1,000 or more. On Magpie, plans are $199 a month for Self-Service with up to 5 visits, $349 for Growth with up to 10 visits, and $699 for Managed with up to 20 visits. There is a 3 month minimum, then month to month, and founding pricing stays locked while you stay subscribed. Founding businesses get $100 off any plan for the first 3 months, so you start at $99, $249, or $599. Your other costs are the comp you choose, and, only if you choose a paid booking, the creator rate plus a 3% card transaction fee.

Do I have to pay creators on top of the plan?

Only if you choose a paid booking. Comped visits are the core of the plan. You host the creator and they post, with no extra creator fee. Paid bookings are optional and use the creator's own listed rate, with a $50 floor. The creator keeps 97% of their rate, and you pay the rate plus a 3% card transaction fee on top.

What does the comp actually cost me?

Whatever you offer. It can be a meal for two, a service, or a package, and you set the comp and its ceiling in the offer. It costs you your cost, not the retail price, because you are paying in your own product or service. Magpie never touches comp money.

Should I comp a creator or pay them? What is the difference between a comped visit and a paid booking?

A comped visit pays the creator in the experience itself. You host a meal, service, or experience at your cost, and the creator posts 1 post and 1 story. A paid booking pays the creator's listed cash rate as well, with money held until the post is live, and it includes one 90 day license for repost and ad use. Comp when the experience is the draw. Pay when you want a specific creator, a specific date, or ad rights.

Why is there a 3-month minimum, and what happens after it?

Because one month of posts is not a marketing strategy, it is a coupon. Creator marketing builds over a season of trusted local posts, and three months is the honest runway to see a pattern. After the minimum, your plan runs month to month. Your founding price stays locked for as long as you stay subscribed.

We have multiple locations. Do we pay per location?

No. One plan covers all your locations. Plans scale by visits per month, 5 on Self-Service, 10 on Growth, and 20 on Managed, and you can use those visits across any of your locations. You do not buy a separate plan for each address.

Booking creators

How do I find and choose the right local creators for my business?

Browse the roster for your market and filter by category, neighborhood, and local audience. Creators are ranked by local reach, not total follower count. Each card shows the local audience share, the local follower count, the creator's rate, whether they accept comps, and their delivery record before you choose. Invite the creators who fit, or, on Managed, let a coordinator pick and schedule for you.

Are a creator's followers actually in my city?

That is the ranking. Every creator card shows their local audience share, the share of followers who live in your market, and the local follower count, before you choose. A creator with 1,500 followers who mostly live here can outrank a much bigger account whose audience lives somewhere else. The local share is self reported until the creator connects Instagram, which verifies it.

How do I tell if a creator's followers are real, not fake or bought?

Magpie does not promise a fake follower audit, and no tool can prove an audience is perfect. What you can see before you book is this. Creators are reviewed before they are listed. Their card shows local audience share and local follower count, ranked by local reach. Their delivery record is public on the card, so you can see whether they show up and post on time. Start with creators who deliver, then rebook the ones who work for you.

Do creators pitch me for free stuff, or do I choose who comes?

You choose who comes. You browse the roster and invite creators, and every visit runs on the same tracked terms. Creators can also pitch you, but only in a limited way. A creator can pitch only if they match your market and categories, pitches are capped, and you can turn pitches off at any time. There is no open inbox of requests for free visits.

What do I need to tell a creator before the visit? Do I have to write a brief?

A short brief in the offer is enough. State what is covered and its ceiling, when to come, where to check in, and any real limits, such as no filming in a treatment room. Add facts a creator needs to get right, such as your name, hours, or price. The deliverable, timing, and disclosure are already in Magpie's booking terms, so both sides start with the same written agreement.

What to expect

What exactly do I get from each visit?

For a comped visit, you get 1 post and 1 story from the creator, posted to their own local audience, honest and disclosed, with the goal of posting within 48 hours of the visit. The post stays up for at least 30 days. Delivered posts save to your content library, and the visit is tracked as checked in, posts live, and redeemed using the creator's code.

What if a creator does not post, posts late, or does not show up?

The goal is a post within 48 hours of the visit. A post inside a week still counts as delivered, just late, and late posts affect the creator's delivery record. If a creator does not show up, or no post appears within a week, that visit does not count against your plan and is replaced. Your plan includes up to 5 visits a month on Self-Service, 10 on Growth, and 20 on Managed. On a paid booking, money is held until the post is live, and you are refunded in full if the visit or post never lands.

Can a creator trash my business? Can I require a positive post?

No. Creators post honest experiences, and you cannot require a positive post. In practice, good experiences get good posts. If a post is factually false or breaks the rules, you can flag it and Magpie reviews it with both sides. Honest opinions are not removed for being unflattering.

Can I tell a creator what to say, or approve the post before it goes live?

You can share facts and boundaries, and you should. Put your correct name, hours, prices, and anything that must not be filmed in the offer. You cannot script the creator's opinion, require specific praise, or demand endless revisions, and you do not approve the post before it goes live. If something factual is wrong, such as a name, price, or hours, asking for that correction is fair.

What is the refund policy?

Paid bookings are held until the post is live and refunded in full if the visit or post never lands. If Magpie cannot deliver, including a founding market that does not open, founding businesses get a full refund. Plans run month to month after the 3 month minimum.

Proof and results

How do I know it worked? How do I track the return?

Your dashboard tracks every booking in three steps, checked in, posts live, and redeemed, using the creator's code. You can see which visits happened, which posts are live, and which offers were redeemed at your register. Magpie counts what the codes and register record. It does not promise a set number of sales or walk ins from a post.

Can Magpie count visits at my register automatically?

Yes, if your register is Square. Connect Square in your account, and creator codes run as Square discounts. Magpie reads redemptions back from Square with the real register dollars, and the register number counts if it ever differs from a hand tally. Without Square, your staff log codes in the app, and those redemptions count the same way.

What is micro-influencer marketing, and is a small creator right for a small business?

Micro influencer marketing means working with smaller creators instead of big national accounts. On Magpie, that is mainly Neighbors with 500 to 2,000 followers and Locals with 2,000 to 15,000, concentrated in your city. For a small business, that is often the right fit, because their followers are nearby customers. Magpie shows each creator's local audience share before you choose, so a smaller local creator can outrank a bigger account from somewhere else.

Posts and rights

Can I reuse a creator's post in my own marketing and ads?

Delivered posts save to your content library. On a comped visit, you can share the post natively, with credit, but you cannot run it as an ad without a license. To use a comped post in ads, send the creator a license request at their listed licensing fee. It covers 90 days, the creator approves it, and the creator keeps 97% of the fee. A paid booking already includes one 90 day license for repost and ad use. Creators own their content.

Who owns the creator's post? What does "in perpetuity" mean if I see it elsewhere?

The creator owns their post. Magpie licenses are limited, not in perpetuity. Perpetuity would mean forever, and Magpie does not sell that. A paid booking includes one license for 90 days of repost and ad use. A comped post is native share only unless you buy a separate 90 day license the creator approves. When a license ends, ad and repost use under that license ends, while the creator's original post stays theirs.

Do creators have to disclose that I comped or paid them? What are the rules for my business?

Yes. A comp or payment is a material connection, so the post must disclose it clearly. The common shorthand is #gifted, and the disclosure should be in the caption or on screen, not hidden. Disclosure is built into Magpie's booking terms, so creators agree to it before the visit. It protects the creator, protects your business, and makes the post more trustworthy to followers.
