The play

The Bench, Not the Scramble

Built before you need it. Here is how a standing music calendar runs itself.

/ 01

Pick your nights once

Choose your standing slots once: every Tuesday early set, Friday nights, Sunday brunch. They live in the same booking system you use for creator visits. No new tool to learn, no weekly booking chore.

/ 02

We build your bench

Magpie matches four to six local musicians to your room: your crowd, your volume, your price range. They rotate through the standing slots, so regulars hear a fresh set and the calendar fills itself. A bench of five cannot quit on you the way one house band can.

/ 03

Every night posts, every guest counts

Each musician makes the standard post after the night, with the goal of posting within 48 hours of the visit, to an audience that actually lives here, so every night keeps marketing the room all week. Attach an audience offer to the night and the guests who come in on it are counted, at your register, on pickup, on delivery.

Covered, twice

A cancellation is a tap, not a crisis. The Swap Bench, our matched stand-in system, offers the closest matched musician from your bench, same week, one tap to rebook. And the Visit Guarantee sits behind the night: a musician who does not show does not burn the booking; it is replaced and does not count against your plan.

Honest FAQ

Straight answers, no sales talk

What does a standing music night cost?

Your plan covers the booking system, the bench, and the proof. A weekly night with a four-musician rotation is about four bookings a month, which fits inside Self-Service at $199 a month, with $100 off your first month and your founding rate held while your plan stays active. Musicians you book paid set their own rates, shown before you choose, and there is no per-night agency percentage on top.

What if a musician cancels, or just does not show?

The bench is the backup. The Swap Bench, our matched stand-in system, offers the closest matched musician from your bench, same week, one tap to rebook. And the Visit Guarantee sits behind the night: a musician who does not show does not burn the booking; it is replaced and does not count against your plan.

Who owns the music?

The musician, always. A booking licenses the post about the night, never the recordings, never the songs. Paid bookings include one standard license on the post itself: 90 days of repost and paid ad use, agreed in the booking terms. Nothing is ever taken in perpetuity.

How do I know the music nights are working?

Attach an audience offer to the night, and guests who come in on a musician's post are counted wherever the order lands: at the counter, on pickup orders, on delivery orders. A Came-Back Pass counts how many of those guests returned within 30 days and paid full price. At signup you give one baseline number, your usual crowd for that night, and your receipt reads the music nights against it: counted floors, the method printed, never inflated.

Why would good musicians say yes?

Because nobody takes a layer off their night. Musicians set their own rate and keep 97 percent of paid bookings, the whole fee is shown in dollars up front, and their money is held safely and paid the day their post goes live, not weeks later. Steady rooms and on-time pay are why the bench stays full.
